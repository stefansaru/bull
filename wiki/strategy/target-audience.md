# Target audience

Who the book is for, what they struggle with, and what they'll pay. Derived from
[[market-research]]; feeds [[vision-mission-objectives]] and content priorities.
Sizes are triangulated estimates (no hard per-segment data exists [16][25]).

## The shape of the market

A **barbell**: a tiny, wealthy, expert **core** (specialists, dealers, expertizers,
institutions, investors — will pay premium) and a broader, shallower **penumbra**
(diaspora/heritage + general/topical collectors — pay less, but far more of them).
Existing works (German Heimbüchler, Romanian Dragomir) serve the *core* and ignore the
*penumbra*. **The book's growth opportunity is the penumbra**, reached by an
accessible, culturally-framed, English/Romanian treatment — without losing the core,
who need the plating/forgery depth.

## Personas

| Persona | Size | Sophistication | Pays | Primary need |
|---------|------|----------------|------|--------------|
| **P1 — Classic-Romania specialists** | dozens–low hundreds (ARGE Rumänien: 27 active [24]) | very high | €150–€300+ | plating, census, forgery, expertise-grade detail |
| **P2 — Dealers & auction houses** (Feldman, Corinphila, Köhler, Gärtner, Spink; Artmark, Timbrofila) [3][8] | global handful | very high | business expense, multiple copies | lotting, description, authentication |
| **P3 — Expertizers / authenticators** (cert-signers) [2][3] | very small | highest | high | definitive forgery/genuineness criteria |
| **P4 — Museums & national libraries** [25] | institutional | curatorial | library budget | reference acquisition, provenance |
| **P5 — Diaspora & heritage buyers** (aurochs = national emblem of RO & MD) [4][26] | **largest by headcount** | low–moderate philatelic, high emotional | €40–€150 | the story; national pride; a beautiful object |
| **P6 — Investors** [11] | small, monied | variable | high | provenance, valuation, authentication of €5k–€100k assets |
| **P7 — General / topical philatelists** (APS ~24,000 [17]; aurochs/animals-on-stamps) | large penumbra | moderate | €40–€100 | curiosity, "famous rarities," accessible entry |

## Pains → who feels them → how the book answers

| Pain (see [[market-research]]) | Felt most by | Book's answer |
|---|---|---|
| Forgery minefield (Sperati/Fournier/Moroiu; museum scandal) | P1 P2 P3 P6 | The missing **modern English forgery/authentication** section, image-heavy |
| Certificate dependency & cost | P1 P6 P7 | Diagnostics + handstamp taxonomy to **pre-screen** before paying for certs |
| Plating difficulty (hand-set single die) | P1 P2 | **Plate-by-plate** per denomination, die-state progression, 1000+ images |
| Fragmented, OOP, language-locked sources | all | **One consolidated volume**; removes the DE/RO language barrier |
| Condition / paper / colour attribution | P1 P2 P7 | Reference apparatus (papers, colours, natural-vs-damage) + colour plates |
| Valuation & census disagreement | P2 P6 P7 | Catalogue **concordance** + census presented as a `debated` range |
| Scattered / lost provenance | P1 P2 P4 P6 | Consolidated **provenance** narrative |
| No cultural/heritage framing | P5 P7 | **Aurochs** natural + cultural history; national-symbol story |

## Implications for the book

1. **Two reading depths in one work.** A narrative a newcomer (P5/P7) enjoys, plus
   specialist apparatus (plating tables, forgery diagnostics, concordances) the core
   (P1–P3) trusts. The wiki's narrative/entity split supports exactly this: prose for
   the story, entity pages for the depth.
2. **Forgery & authentication is the killer feature** — it is the biggest unserved pain
   and spans the paying core. Prioritise it in [[../meta/requirements|REQ-C7]].
3. **Heritage framing unlocks the largest headcount** (P5) — protect the aurochs
   throughline (C-16) and the Romanian edition.
4. **English widens the paying + institutional + awards reach**; German matches the
   incumbent scholarship; Romanian serves heritage — which is exactly the language
   ordering in [[multilanguage-analysis]] (French adds least).
5. **Trust is the currency.** Referencing every claim (DR-007) and being honest about
   debates (DR-006) is not academic nicety — it is what converts P1–P4 and P6.
