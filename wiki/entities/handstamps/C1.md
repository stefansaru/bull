# ⨀ C1 — „RECEPISSE” registered-mail mark of Moldavia (1857)

**`C1`** is the straight-line **"RECEPISSE"** handstamp — a **complementary**
marking (not an obliterator) applied to **registered** letters to show the sender
had a receipt of dispatch (*recepisse*). It is the counterpart of the datestamp
[[M1]]: on registered mail the two travel together, `M1` cancelling the stamp and
`C1` marking the service.

## History

The `C1` "RECEPISSE" marks were **ordered in 1857 at Vienna** from the engraver
**J. Radnitzky**, at the initiative of **Teodor Ghica** for the dispatch service of
the Moldavian Diligence Company he administered — the same commission and inventory
(**10 November 1857**) as the [[M1]] datestamps. Their use was **mandatory in the 14
dispatch offices** on registered, prepaid letters, phased in from **Iași, 22 July
1858**, twelve further Moldavian offices on **4 August 1858**, and **Galați on 1
September 1858**. They were withdrawn by **Order no. 3147 of 1/13 June 1865** and
replaced by the **`C7` "RECOMANDAT"** marks introduced from 11 February 1865
(narrative ch. *C1*, after [[../references/catalogues/kiriac-1974|Kiriac 1974]] pp. 34, 261).

`C1` normally accompanies `M1` rather than cancelling the stamp; it obliterates a
stamp only exceptionally — a noted example being a **108-parale** fragment offered at
**Harmers, London, 1991** (Kiriac fig. 265, p. 294). A later frame-size variant is
catalogued as **`C1a` (RECEPISSE, 1859)**.
