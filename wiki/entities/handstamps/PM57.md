# 🐂 PM57 — Pre-philatelic "MOLDOVA" dated town handstamps (from 1857)

The author's code **`PM57`** (Dragomir §1.1.2, *"Ștampilele prefilatelice
'MOLDOVA'"*) denotes the **dated round "MOLDOVA" town datestamps** of the Moldavian
post in their **pre-philatelic use, from 1857** — i.e. *before* the *Cap de bour*
stamps of 22 July 1858
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photos 115, 118).

**These are the same physical devices later catalogued as [[M1]]** (the small round
"MOLDOVA" obliterators, Planșa 18, Fig. 271–286). Dragomir's PM57 section carries
**no plate or figures of its own**: it describes the marks as *"ștampile
`M1`.MOLDOVA"* and points to Planșa 18 and to the illustrating letter **Fig. 287**
(which lives in the M1 chapter)
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 118; cf.
Planșa 18, photo 139). `PM57` is therefore best read as the **pre-stamp period label**
for the M1 dies, distinguishing their 1857 use from their post-1858 role as stamp
obliterators.

## History — Vienna, 1857

The dies were **commissioned and paid for by Teodor Balș** and **engraved at Vienna
by J. Radnitzky**, on the occasion of the spring-1857 contracting of new diligences
and equipment for the Moldavian expedition bureaus; the state "bought back" the
carriages and *"ștampilele datate"* (dated handstamps) in **November 1857**
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 115). (The
[[M1]] page gives the same provenance under the diligence entrepreneur **Teodor
Ghica** and the **10 November 1857** inventory — see the inconsistency note below.)

**Device.** Double-circle, roughly **21.5 × 12.2 mm**, with the **post-office
locality above and "MOLDOVA" below** in the exergue, the two words separated by a
short dash (11 mm) — the description of the M1 series
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] Planșa 18, photos
138–139).

## The bureaus known in pre-philatelic use

Dragomir lists **nine** bureaus (German-form names) whose "MOLDOVA" datestamps fall
in the pre-philatelic category
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 118):

**1. BAKEU · 2. BOTUSCHANI · 3. DOROHOJ · 4. GALATI · 5. HUSCH · 6. JASSY ·
7. MICHALENI · 8. PIATRA · 9. WASLUI.**

**Five surviving pre-philatelic examples** are recorded, all struck in **1857**:

| Strike | Date | Provenance |
|--------|------|------------|
| JASSY | 15/4 (earliest recorded) | col. G. Sassower |
| JASSY | 22/12 | Lic. Köhler, Oct. 1978 |
| GALATZ | 23/12 | col. L. N. Cristea |
| JASSY | 24/6 | **Fig. 287**, col. L. N. Cristea |
| WASLUI | 4/11 | col. D. Passalega |

([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 118.) The
book also flags that the **Tg. Neamț** expedition bureau received a die ("cutia cu
cifrele de alamă") — a fifteenth office beyond Planșa 18's fourteen — and asks that
its existence be traced further (photo 118).

## ⚠ Notes on the source (verify)

- **PM57 ≡ M1 (same devices, two codes).** The section describes the marks as
  `M1`.MOLDOVA and reuses the M1 plate/figure; treat `PM57` as the pre-stamp label,
  `M1` as the obliterator label, for one set of dies. See [[M1]].
- **Provenance names differ.** PM57 credits **Teodor Balș** (photo 115); the M1
  chapter credits **Teodor Ghica**, the diligence entrepreneur (photo 139). ⚠ (OCR —
  verify against a clean copy).
- The running text mixes a count of **nine** prefilatelic MOLDOVA marks with a list
  of **six** stamps "missing / in delivery" (Bacău, Fălticeni, Focșani, Roman, …,
  Tg. Neamț) — numbers not fully reconciled ⚠ (OCR — verify) (photo 118).
