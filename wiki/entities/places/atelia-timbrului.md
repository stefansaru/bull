# Atelia Timbrului — the Stamp Workshop, Iași

> Every genuine 1858 *cap de bour* was born in one small room in Iași. Not a mint, not a
> security printer — a **fiscal-stamp workshop** with a single hand press whose table held
> exactly one sheet at a time. This page is about that place: how it came to exist, the four
> steel dies it was given, and the slow, one-stamp-at-a-time method that stamped Moldavia's
> first postage — and that, by making no two impressions alike, is the reason the whole issue
> can be **plated** today. For die/cliché/tête-bêche terms see [[../../meta/terminology]].

## What it was

The **Stamp Workshop** — Romanian *Atelia Timbrului*, abbreviated **A.T.** in the archival
record — was a printing office of the Ministry of Finance in **Iași (Jassy)**, the capital of
Moldavia. It was **established in January 1856 by Prince Grigore Ghica** for the purpose of
printing the so-called **"Princely Stamp"** (*Timbru Domnesc*), the embossed/impressed fiscal
stamp used on official documents ([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974 Ch.I (EN)]] p.29).
By 1858, when it was tasked with the postage stamps, the workshop **had already been printing
the Timbrul Domnesc for two years**, so the *cap de bour* was grafted onto an existing fiscal
operation rather than a purpose-built stamp press ([[../../narrative/04-1858/023-1858-1-printing-process-iaşi-jassy|Printing process]]).

The workshop was headed by a **Secretary** — the Ministry of Finance addressed its 1 July 1858
notice to "His Honour the Secretary of the Stamp Workshop" in Jassy ([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974 Ch.I (EN)]] p.28).
It sat within a wider postal apparatus that was reorganised in Iași that same spring: on
1 May 1858 the **Directorate of Posts and Stagecoaches (D.P.D.)** and the Central Postal Office
were set up under the Ministry of Finance, the D.P.D. supervising postal operations while the
Central Office handled dispatch ([[../../narrative/04-1858/018-1858-dpd-and-central-postal-office-iaşi-jassy|D.P.D. and Central Postal Office]]).
The workshop was the **manufacturer**; the D.P.D. was the customer that sold and cancelled the
finished stamps.

## Equipment: the press bought from Vienna

To print the Princely Stamp the workshop equipped itself with a proper press. In **March 1857
a new "press with balance-lever and counter" was bought from Vienna**, and to install it "a
table with iron screws" was made locally in the country
([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974 Ch.I (EN)]] p.29).
Because that press was acquired in 1857 as an addition, the workshop held **at least one older
press** as well ([[../../narrative/04-1858/023-1858-1-printing-process-iaşi-jassy|Printing process]]).
The essential feature of the machine was a **rotating mechanism that pressed a metal die
vertically down onto a sheet of paper**: the die was screwed to a support called a *printing
plate*, which travelled on a vertical axis down onto the sheet lying on a **small work table
that could hold only one sheet at a time** ([[../../narrative/04-1858/023-1858-1-printing-process-iaşi-jassy|Printing process]]).

## The decision to make postage stamps

The commissioning of the *cap de bour* ran through the Ministry of Finance in Iași across the
spring and summer of 1858:

- **1 May 1858** — the D.P.D. asked the Ministry of Finance (request **no. 975**) to have the
  stamps manufactured, in conformity with the "Instructions for the Conveyance of Letters",
  naming the first three values (27, 54, 81 parale); the fourth value, 108 parale, was added
  later ([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974 Ch.I (EN)]] p.28;
  [[../../narrative/04-1858/019-1858-1-manufacturing-request-iaşi-jassy|Manufacturing Request]]).
- **26 May 1858** — the Ministry of Finance decided (**no. 4258**) that the stamps should be
  **printed at the Stamp Workshop in Iași**, and approved the ordering of "the dies for those
  stamps" ([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974 Ch.I (EN)]] p.28;
  [[../../narrative/04-1858/020-1858-1-manufacturing-decision-iaşi-jassy|Manufacturing Decision]]).

## The four round steel dies (clichés)

On **1 July 1858** the Ministry of Finance informed the Secretary of the Stamp Workshop
(communication **no. 5895**) that **"four requisite dies had been manufactured"**, one per value
([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974 Ch.I (EN)]] p.28;
[[../../narrative/04-1858/021-1858-1-metal-dies-iaşi-jassy|Metal Dies]]).
The clichés (Kiriac's Fig. 37) are:

- **round in shape**, and **engraved in steel** ([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974 Ch.I (EN)]] p.28);
- each carrying **two grooves**, by means of which they were **fixed into the head (support)
  of the printing machine** — i.e. clamped to the printing plate that the press lowered onto
  the paper ([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974 Ch.I (EN)]] pp.28–29;
  [[../../narrative/04-1858/021-1858-1-metal-dies-iaşi-jassy|Metal Dies]]).

**The manufacture of the clichés cost 389 lei**
([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974 Ch.I (EN)]] p.29;
[[../../narrative/04-1858/021-1858-1-metal-dies-iaşi-jassy|Metal Dies]]).
Because engraving was expensive and the four values share fine details, it is highly likely
that the **same set of dies** served **both** the first and second Moldavian issues — which is
why authentication rests on close inspection of the specific design details a given die
imprints ([[../../narrative/04-1858/023-1858-1-printing-process-iaşi-jassy|Printing process]]).
(For the second issue in October 1858 the workshop again "requested the dies" from the Ministry
of Finance, [[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974 Ch.I (EN)]] p.33.)

## How the sheet was printed — one impression at a time

The press was **not modified** for the new job: the Princely Stamp was still being printed on it,
so the *cap de bour* had to be produced with the **existing set-up and the same technique already
used to print a single "wet" fiscal stamp in the corner of a page**
([[../../narrative/04-1858/023-1858-1-printing-process-iaşi-jassy|Printing process]]).
This manual process is documented in Wertheimer-Ghika (1944)
([[../../narrative/04-1858/023-1858-1-printing-process-iaşi-jassy|Printing process]]).
The operation went as follows ([[../../narrative/04-1858/023-1858-1-printing-process-iaşi-jassy|Printing process]]):

1. A **single die** was mounted on the printing plate and stayed **fixed in one place** on it.
2. A sheet was laid on the small table; the operator lowered the plate until the die touched the
   paper, transferring one inked impression, then raised it and **nudged the sheet forward** for
   the next stamp in the column.
3. This was repeated **eight times** down a column, then the sheet shifted sideways for a second
   adjacent column of eight — both columns in the **same orientation**.
4. The table was too small to move the sheet further sideways, so to print the remaining columns
   **the sheet was turned upside down**; the third and fourth columns were therefore printed
   **inverted (reversed)** relative to the first two.

Because the lowered plate **blocked the operator's view**, placement was judged by estimate;
spacing between stamps varies, and stamps were sometimes printed too close together or even
**overlapping** ([[../../narrative/04-1858/023-1858-1-printing-process-iaşi-jassy|Printing process]];
[[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974 Ch.I (EN)]] p.29).
Kiriac summarises the whole method as **typographed "with single-cliché dies"**
([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974 Ch.I (EN)]] p.42).

## The tête-bêche sheet

The finished sheet held **four rows of eight stamps** (32 stamps). Read along a horizontal, the
**upper two rows are normal** (bull's head with muzzle downward) while the **lower two rows are
inverted** (bull turned through 180°, muzzle up, horns down). At the centre the sheet thus formed
**eight pairs of stamps turned horn-to-horn**, the arrangement known in philately as
**tête-bêche** ([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974 Ch.I (EN)]] p.29;
[[../../meta/terminology]]).
The tête-bêche pairing was **a direct consequence of the printing limitation** — the need to
turn the sheet over to reach the remaining columns — not a deliberate design
([[../../narrative/04-1858/023-1858-1-printing-process-iaşi-jassy|Printing process]]).
A related side-effect: the workshop's printing was done by **two employees, one experienced and
one a novice**, and on some surviving sheets the outer columns (first and fourth) show a
**different shade of colour**, suggesting they were printed at different times — hence the
well-known vertical pairs with one stamp darker than the other
([[../../narrative/04-1858/023-1858-1-printing-process-iaşi-jassy|Printing process]]).

### Why plating is possible

Because **each stamp was struck separately** from a die fixed in one spot, and placement was by
eye, **every impression on the sheet differs slightly** in position, spacing and inking. The
individual design idiosyncrasies each die imprints are therefore the basis for authenticating —
and reconstructing (plating) — the issue ([[../../narrative/04-1858/023-1858-1-printing-process-iaşi-jassy|Printing process]];
see [[../postmarks/kiriac-figures]] for Kiriac's die and design plates, and
[[../issues/issue-bull-1]] for the issue itself).

## Output: ~24,000 stamps

The Ministry of Finance's 1 July 1858 notice specified that **24,000 stamps** were to be printed
from the four dies, apportioned "in accordance with the tariffs drawn up":
**6,000 of 27 parale; 10,000 of 54 parale; 2,000 of 81 parale; 6,000 of 108 parale**
([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974 Ch.I (EN)]] p.28;
[[../../narrative/04-1858/021-1858-1-metal-dies-iaşi-jassy|Metal Dies]]).
The workshop delivered the stamps to the treasury of the Ministry of Finance in **two batches**:

- **First batch, 11 July 1858** (A.T. document no. 62): **187 sheets = 5,984 stamps** (992 × 27p;
  992 × 54p; 480 × 81p; 3,520 × 108p), so that stamping of private letters could begin from
  15 July ([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974 Ch.I (EN)]] p.29;
  [[../../narrative/04-1858/024-1858-1-first-batch-delivered-from-at-to-mf-iaşi-jassy|First batch delivered from A.T. to M.F.]]).
- **Second (final) batch, 21 July 1858** (A.T. Report 64): the remaining **18,080 stamps**
  ([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974 Ch.I (EN)]] p.29).

Kiriac's own print-run table totals **24,064 stamps in 752 sheets** — the "24,000" of the order
being a round planning figure ([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974 Ch.I (EN)]] p.30).
Locally, a Botoșani account confirms that it was at the **"Atelia Timbrului" in Iași** that the
four values first "saw the light of print", the first cancelled use falling on **22 July 1858**
([[../../sources/botosani-cap-de-bour/text|Poșta și filateliștii botoșăneni]] p.1).

## Afterward: the move to Bucharest

The workshop's Moldavian chapter ended with unification. In **December 1861 / January 1862 the
Stamp Workshop began moving its equipment, inventory and specialist personnel from Iași to
Bucharest**, where it resumed work under the name **"Autograph Press" (*Presa Autografă*)**,
still under the Ministry of Finance ([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974 Ch.I (EN)]] p.36).
The same "old manual press" and single-die, hand-press method carried over to the first
"United Principalities" stamps of 1862 before machine printing arrived in 1864
([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974 Ch.I (EN)]] pp.37–38).
