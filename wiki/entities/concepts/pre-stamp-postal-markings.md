# Before the stamp — postal seals and handstamps of the Principalities, 1822–1858

The *Cap de bour* of July 1858 was the first **adhesive** the letter-writer could
buy, but it was not the first mark the post put on a letter. For more than a
generation before it, the mail of **Moldavia** and **Wallachia (Țara Românească)**
had been struck by **seals and handstamps** — devices held by the relay houses,
the postal directorates, the quarantine stations and the town bureaus. This page is
the anchor for that pre-stamp world: what the devices were, how they are read and
classified, and how the story runs in **parallel across the two principalities**
from **1822** to the reform of 1858. The single indispensable source is Kiriac
Dragomir's *Ștampilografie Poștală (România: 1822–1910)*
([[../references/books/dragomir-stampilografie-1990]]); its taxonomy and device codes are
used throughout.

## Sigiliu, pecete, ștampilă — what the devices were

Dragomir's field is **sigilografie** (sigillography) applied to the post: the study
of the **sigiliu** (seal/matrix) and its impression. He distinguishes the terms
carefully. The same matrix, pressed **into wax**, gives a *pecete* (wax seal);
applied **in soot/ink** (*în fum*) it gives a **ștampilă sigilară** (seal handstamp);
a matrix cut in negative yields a *ștampilă negativă*
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 10). The
oldest Romanian postal markings are therefore **seals**, not the round datestamps of
later philately — they authenticated a document and identified the office that sent
it.

Every impression is read as two parts, **emblemă + legendă** (emblem + legend). The
emblem is the symbolic image; the legend is the surrounding text, set at the margin
(sometimes between perled lines, in the *exergă*), running **clockwise from the
left**. The earliest legends are in the **Romanian Cyrillic** alphabet, giving way
over the period to Latin letters
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 10).

## Two principalities, two emblems

The first Romanian seals are **heraldic**, and here the pre-stamp record already
carries the motif the whole wiki turns on. **"The principal element of the heraldic
seals of Moldavia is the *cap de bour* (bull's head), and of Wallachia the *acvila*
(eagle, 'the vulture' in philatelic usage)."** Dragomir draws the line to the stamps
explicitly: the bull's head *"appears for the first time in philately on the cap de
bour stamps"* — the first issue of 22 July 1858 and the second of 1 November 1858 —
while the eagle *"appears for the first time beside the bull's head"* on the later
*Principatele Unite* stamps
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 10). The
aurochs head that makes the 1858 stamps famous was thus, for thirty-six years
already, the working emblem of the Moldavian post's own seals. See
[[moldavia-heraldry]] and [[aurochs-symbolism]].

## The relay houses: where the record begins, 1822

The catalogue opens with the **seals of the two menzil houses**, and this is where
the pre-stamp story joins [[menzil-relay-system|the menzil relay post]].

- **Moldavia — *Casa Menzilului Moldovei*, 1822–1834.** Its earliest known seals show
  a mounted courier (*surugiu / timiraș*) between trees, sounding a horn or holding
  the reins and whip, within a double frame, the Cyrillic legend giving *"PECETEA
  KĂSII MENZILULUI MOLDAVIEI"* and the year: **`SM22`** (1822), **`SM27`** (*Casa
  Menzilului*, 1827, double circle 33/24 mm, with a mounted postillion sounding a
  horn between two trees), **`SM30`** (1830)
  ([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photos 11–12).
  `SM22` survives on a single known document — a postal order of 7 September 1825 to
  the Dorohoi post, under the Scufa lease of 1822/25 — *"exemplar unic"*, in the
  collection of Fritz Heimbüchler (photo 11).
- **Wallachia — *Casa Menzilurilor Valahiei*.** The parallel series of Wallachian
  seals runs `SV27` (Casa Menzilurilor Valahiei), `SV30`, `SV33` (Casa Poștelor),
  `SV46` (Direcția Poștelor), and the seals of the individual post stations gathered
  in Dragomir's Plate 7
  ([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photos 30–32).

These are catalogued in office-tables (`TABEL I` for Moldavia lists the sigilary
seals of ~23 bureaus, 1822–1858 — Direcția, Bacău, Bârlad, Bolgrad, Botoșani, Cahul,
Dorohoi, Fălticeni, Focșani, Galați, Huși, Iași, Ismail, and more) (photo 13).

## How Dragomir classifies them

The pre-stamp markings fall into three families by relation to the adhesive stamp
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photos 8, 10):

1. **Sigilare** (`SM` Moldavia, `SV` Wallachia) — the office **seals**, in use from
   1822; the oldest layer.
2. **Prefilatelice** (`P`; composite `PM` Moldavia, `PV` Wallachia) — town and office
   handstamps used **before** the stamp existed.
3. **Filatelice** — the obliterators that cancelled the stamps once they appeared:
   for Moldavia, the small **`M1`** (1857), the large **`M2`** (1859) and the
   **`M3`** *Franco obliterante* (1859).

Cutting across these are the **service** classes: the **`FM`/`FV`** road-post
**transport-form seals** (§1.3 — the eagle-and-post-cart devices struck on the leased
post's paperwork; despite the code letter, *not* the "FRANCO" prepayment marks — see
[[franco-porto-handstamps]]), the **`K`** **quarantine** offices (*carantine* —
sanitary cordons where letters from plague- and cholera-country were disinfected and
struck — see [[quarantine-posts]]), the true *FRANCO*/*PORTO* prepayment/accounting
marks ([[franco-porto-apparatus-1858]]), and the maritime, river and rural services.
The full key is tabulated in [[../references/books/dragomir-stampilografie-1990#the-classification-system-dragomirs-codes|the reference page]].

## Why it belongs in this book

The pre-stamp markings are the **evidential floor** under the *Cap de bour* story.
They show the Moldavian post already carrying the bull's head; they document the
offices, routes and quarantine cordons the 1858 stamps then travelled through; and
their obliterators (`M1`/`M2`/`M3`) are the very marks found tying the first and
second Bull's-Head issues to a place and date — the material of
[[bull-head-postmarks-from-documents]] and the [[../postmarks/census|postmark
census]]. The reform that produced the adhesive stamp is told in
[[postal-history-romania]]; the machinery it replaced, in [[menzil-relay-system]].
