# 📅 Gregorian calendar (G, New Style)

Introduced in 1582 to correct the drift of the [[julian-calendar|Julian calendar]],
the Gregorian calendar changed the leap-year rule so the year stays aligned with the
sun. Catholic Europe adopted it at once (Austria in 1583); the Orthodox world kept the
Julian calendar much longer — the Ottoman Empire changed in 1917 (fully in 1926),
Russia in 1918 and Romania in 1919
([[../../narrative/21-calendar/001-calendar|Calendar]]).

For the Bull's Head years this means that foreign sources — Austrian post offices,
Western newspapers, auction catalogues — usually give Gregorian dates, while
Moldavian documents give Julian ones. In the 19th century the Gregorian date is the
Julian date **plus 12 days**.

How both dates are written together: [[dual-date-notation]].
