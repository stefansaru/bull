# Franco and porto — the documentary apparatus of the 1858 post

A postage stamp is only the visible token of a much larger machine. For the
Moldavian [[postal-history-romania|reformed post of 1858]] that machine was a
body of registers, waybills, receipts and internal reports — most of it
handwritten in Cyrillic — through which every letter was taxed, entered,
sorted, dispatched and accounted for. Wertheimer-Ghika's 1944 study reproduces
this paperwork directly from the author's collection and from the surviving
Directorate files, and it is that documentary base, more than the stamps
themselves, that shows how the *Cap de bour* ["Bull's Head"](../issues/issue-bull-1)
values actually worked and how a genuine usage can be told from a fabrication.
This page walks through those documents:
the **franco / porto** distinction that organised the whole system, the
**register** that recorded it, and the individual document **types** the book
photographs.

## 1. Franco versus porto

The reform gave the public two ways to pay. A letter could be prepaid at posting
by affixing stamps — **franco** — or it could travel unpaid and be charged to
the addressee on delivery — **porto** (postage due). The book states the
distinction almost as a caption to the special handstamps struck for money and
parcel dispatches: *"Cele cu «Franco» serveau pentru trimiterile plătite la
plecare; cele cu «Porto» pentru trimiteri plătibile la sosire"* — those marked
*Franco* served for items **paid at departure**, those marked *Porto* for items
**payable on arrival**
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 37,
p.99). Both **FRANCO** and **PORTO** existed as town-named linear handstamps —
e.g. *FRANCO BOTUSANI*, *PORTO BOTOSANI* — alongside the columns for weight, tax
and seal (*pondera, taxa, pecete*) used on money and parcel forwardings
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 37).
The same pair of words was even built into the design of the later stamps: when
the alphabet was Latinised the new values carried the inscriptions **"Porto
Scrisoarei"** (letter postage) and **"Porto Gazetei"** (newspaper postage) round
their margins, a deliberate step to spread the Latin letters through the public
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 39).

Because the franco option was what the adhesive stamps served, the whole point
of the Bull's Heads was to let a sender discharge the tariff *at plecare*; the
porto column is the record of everything that was **not** prepaid and had to be
collected in cash — the accounting counterweight that made the stamp figures
meaningful.

## 2. The register: *Jurnalul scrisorilor franco-porto*

At the heart of the office lay a bound register, the **"Jurnalul scrisorilor
franco-porto"** (the journal of franco/porto letters), which the book reproduces
from the author's own collection. Letters handed in for dispatch were *"zilnic
înscrise în «Jurnalul scrisorilor franco-porto» … subîmpărțit după localitățile
de destinație"* — entered **daily**, the register being **sub-divided by
destination town**, with a separate column for the amount owed by the addressee
(*porto*); once entered they were made up into the packet for that route
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 50).
The heading itself appears in Cyrillic capitals on the photographed page
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 50).

A second, related book was kept at the receiving end: the **"Jurnalul porto
scrisorilor"** — "…*sosite de la stațiile următoare spre trădare cu plata*"
(letters **arriving** from the following stations for delivery against payment),
printed like the first journal but holding only the unpaid, no-stamp items that
one office had to collect for another
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 53;
described photo 54, p.101). On the **verso** of the franco-porto journal were
noted registered letters, money-groups (*gropuri cu bani*) and parcels; and
attached to the system was a **"Listă a scrisorilor înapoiate la Direcția
Poștelor"** (list of letters returned to the Directorate) — the specimen
published, from the Focșani office, holds **9 returned letters** with a total
unpaid porto of **8 lei** in March 1860, annotated "letters to the Archive"
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 54).

Accompanying each dispatch was a **factura** (also spelt *fatură*) — *"o
adresă-formular însoțitoare a scrisorilor expediate"*, i.e. a pre-printed
covering address-slip listing the enclosed items
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 50).
Its rubrics divided the batch into **scrisori recomandate** (registered),
**scrisori francate** (prepaid), **scrisori spre trădare de plată** (for delivery
against payment) and transit letters
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 51).
The oldest surviving example the author owned is the covering *factura* of the
St Spiridon foundation's mail on the postal road, distinguishing letters sent
franked (with stamps) from those sent *porto*, without stamps
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 52).

## 3. The document types the book reproduces

Wertheimer-Ghika photographs, and often transcribes, the recurring form-types of
the office. Their headings are frequently mangled by the OCR, so the readings are
noted here:

- **Raport** (report) — by far the commonest heading (*RAPORT*), the standard
  upward communication between an *expediție* (branch office) and the Directorate
  — e.g. the report of the Bolgrad office, or of Bacău to "Direcția Poștelor și a
  Diligențelor"
  ([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photos 44,
  50).
- **Referat** (memorandum / minute) — the internal note or recommendation, often
  the cashier's, on which a *rezoluție* (ruling) was then written
  ([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photos 36,
  49).
- **Scrisoare** (letter) — the item itself; the accompanying *scrisoare
  însoțitoare* announced a money-group's departure while the cash travelled
  separately in numerar
  ([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 37).
- **Chitanță** (receipt) — printed *"CFITANTIE"* in the OCR — issued by the
  Directorate cashier to the Ministry of Finance to acknowledge stamps received,
  e.g. the receipt for 10,816 second-issue values, 27 October 1858
  ([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 44).
- **Recipisă poștală** (postal receipt) — printed *"RECIPISA POSTALA"*, also
  called **adeverință poștală** — the receipt given for **registered letters**
  and for items of declared value, money-groups and parcels, in models running
  1858–1862; the earliest specimen dates from 1855. A variant, the
  **Retur-Recepisă** (return receipt), came back bearing the addressee's
  signature
  ([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photos
  54–57, pp.101–105).
- **Blanchet de poștă** (pre-printed postal form / waybill) — printed *"BLANCHET
  DE POSTA"*, with dated specimens of 1859 and 1863; these were the printed
  station-and-diligence forms filled in by the post captains along a road (*"Capitanii
  poștelor de pe drumul…"*), recording the stage, the plata and the fee per
  station
  ([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photos 66,
  67, pp.125–127).

## 4. The officials in the chain

The apparatus was worked by a small hierarchy. Outside Iași the district
**sameș** — *"samesul, adică casierul districtului"*, the sameș being the
**district cashier** — received stamps from the Directorate and passed them down;
one office (Focșani) then sent on a small stock to its "outside dispatchers"
(*expeditori din afară*)
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 40).
The book states plainly that the *sameși* were "the cashiers of the districts"
who had run the regional offices since 1852
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 26).
Within the central office the **casier** (cashier) reconciled stamps issued and
sold against the receipts (chitanțe), while the **expeditor** — including an
*expeditor central* — handled forwarding
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photos 26,
27). Notably, the passenger post captains (*căpitanii stațiilor de poștă ale
pasagerilor*) were also to "serve as postal dispatchers", so that stations such
as Adjud franked and forwarded private letters
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 40).
*(The term "diriginte" for a postmaster, listed in some later literature, does
not itself appear in these documents, which use **expeditor** / **expediție**;
DR-006.)*

## 5. Why the paperwork is the evidence base

This machinery is what turns a printed label into a working prepaid system — and
it is the reason the study can authenticate usages that earlier authorities had
dismissed as non-existent or false. Because each office's stamp consumption,
franco receipts and porto returns were logged month by month, a cover can be
checked against the record: the book, for instance, reproduces *"o
scrisoare expediată din Iași la Galați, recomandată și grabnică, francată cu 2
bucăți de mărci de 80 parale"* — a **registered, express** letter franked with
two 80-parale stamps — precisely the kind of piece the registers make credible
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 69,
p.131). The survival of the Directorate's own files, the author argues, restored
to Romanian philately some of its rarest genuine cancelled pieces that had been
wrongly declared forgeries when the archive was thought lost
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 46). The
forms are thus not background: they are the primary witnesses for how the
[[bull-head-stamp-distribution-1858|stamps were distributed and used]], and for
distinguishing authentic 1858 usages from later inventions (see
[[../postmarks/kiriac-figures|Kiriac figures]] for the associated handstamps).
