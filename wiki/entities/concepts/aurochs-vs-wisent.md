# Aurochs or wisent? (bour vs. zimbru)

> [!debate] Status: `debated`
> The animal on the arms of Moldavia — and on the 1858 [[../issues/issue-bull-1|Bull's Head]]
> stamps that inherit that emblem — is usually called an **aurochs** (Romanian *bour*,
> *Bos primigenius*). But the heraldic and pictorial record is not tidy: the same frontal
> horned head has repeatedly been read, drawn, and even described as a **wisent / European
> bison** (Romanian *zimbru*, *Bison bonasus*). This page lays out the two identifications
> and the reasons behind each **without deciding between them**. For the animal itself see
> [[aurochs]]; for the emblem's history see [[aurochs-symbolism]] and
> [[moldavia-heraldry]]; for the founding hunt see [[../events/dragos-founding-moldova]].

The ambiguity is not a modern slip. It is baked into the sources, into the Romanian
vocabulary, and into the fact that the emblem outlived the living animal by centuries. Two
different beasts stand behind one silhouette, and telling them apart from a stylised
heraldic head is genuinely hard — which is much of what makes the emblem, and the stamps
that carry it, intriguing.

## Two animals, one silhouette

The **aurochs** (*bour*) is *Bos primigenius*, the wild ox of Eurasia and the direct
ancestor of all domestic cattle; the primary zoological literature calls it "huge and
reputedly fierce" and dates its extinction to 1627, when the last animal died in Poland
[[../../sources/felius-2014-cattle-history/text|Felius 2014]] p.2. In present-day Romania
it was a real, native forest animal, known locally as **"Bour"**
[[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/023-romanian-aurochs|Narrative — Romanian Aurochs]].

The **wisent** (*zimbru*) is *Bison bonasus*, the European bison — one of the two living
bison species alongside the American *Bison bison*
[[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/009-aurochs-bison|Narrative — Aurochs & Bison]]. It, too,
was a large horned bovine of the same Carpathian forests, and medieval hunting records
from the region list the *bour* and the *zimbru* side by side as the great game animals of
the nobility [[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.66. The two
species are, in the words of the book's own narrative, "often confused" because of their
"similar appearance" [[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/011-bison|Narrative — Bison]] ·
[[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/009-aurochs-bison|Narrative — Aurochs & Bison]]. That is
the whole problem in one sentence: a frontal head with a pair of horns can be read either
way. On the *bour / urus / zimbru* vocabulary see [[../../meta/terminology]].

## The case that it is the *bour* (aurochs)

**1. The name of the charge.** In Romanian usage the emblem is the **cap de bour** —
literally "aurochs/ox head." That is how the medieval and early-modern record names it: the
chronicler Miron Costin, writing in 1677, observed that Sighet, the capital of Maramureș,
"to this day bears as its seal the *cap de bour*, just like this country [Moldova]"
[[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.63. The word chosen by the
tradition is the aurochs word, not the bison word.

**2. The founding legend names a *bour*.** In the Dragoș origin story the quarry of the
founding hunt is explicitly an **aurochs** (*bour*), run to its death across the land that
became Moldavia — the animal whose head then enters the arms
[[../events/dragos-founding-moldova]] ·
[[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/025-the-legend-of-dragoş|Narrative — The legend of
Dragoș]]. Whatever its historical value, the legend fixes the emblem's identity as the
aurochs in cultural memory.

**3. The region hunted the aurochs, organised and by name.** The *bour* was real quarry in
this landscape. Tiron documents an organised medieval aurochs-hunting apparatus: a 1208
"decury" of *bour*-hunters (*vânători de bouri*) in Crasna county, and by 1213 an official
styled "count of the aurochs-hunters" (*comite al vânătorilor de bouri*); a 1138 revenue
grant even lists "an aurochs horn" (*cornu bubalinum*) among a monastery's dues
[[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.66. The aurochs, in short,
was the prestige beast a founder-hunter would plausibly take as a personal emblem — the
"superlative" of the royal and seigneurial hunts of 13th–14th-century Maramureș
[[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.66.

On this reading the emblem is, straightforwardly, the head of the animal the tradition says
it is: the *bour*.

## The case (and the confusion) for the *zimbru* (wisent / bison)

**1. Contemporaries themselves read it as a *zimbru*.** The identification is not uniform
even in the primary heraldic literature. Ioan Mihály de Apșa, cataloguing the Maramureș
seals, described a 1421 seal as showing "a **cap de zimbru** [wisent head] with a star
between the horns," and supposed that a *cap de zimbru* had been the mark of the whole
county in the 14th century and earlier
[[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.65. Where the tradition says
*bour*, a careful 19th–20th-century reader of the actual seals reached for *zimbru*.

**2. When the model died, the drawing drifted.** The strongest structural reason for the
bison reading is chronological. The aurochs became extinct in 1627
[[../../sources/felius-2014-cattle-history/text|Felius 2014]] p.2 — but the emblem lived
on for centuries afterward, on seals, coins and eventually stamps. With no living aurochs
left to look at, artists had a surviving large horned bovine to hand: the wisent. The
book's own narrative states this plainly: "Mostly due to the extinction of the aurochs,
people began representing the wisent (European bison) instead in many instances," which
"led to some confusion between the bison and the wisent, as the two species were often
mistakenly interchanged"
[[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/002-symbol-of-moldavia|Narrative — Symbol of Moldavia]].
The emblem can therefore be "an aurochs" by intention and "a bison" by execution at the
same time.

**3. The 1858 stamps sit right on the fault line.** The confusion is explicitly attached to
the [[../issues/issue-bull-1|1858 issue]]: "The aurochs and the European bison are often
confused, especially in discussions of the 1858 stamps," the narrative notes, adding that
the practical way to tell them apart is "the shape and size of their antlers [horns]"
[[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/011-bison|Narrative — Bison]]. A modern commemorative even
places the European bison (*Bison bonasus*) alongside the aurochs in a stamp design honoring
the 1858 anniversary [[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/011-bison|Narrative — Bison]] — the
two animals travelling together, still not fully distinguished.

## The biological differences that (should) settle it — and why they don't

If one could see the animal clearly, the two species are distinguishable. The trouble is
that a heraldic head shows exactly the traits that a stylised drawing blurs.

- **Horns.** This is the single feature the sources put forward as decisive: the aurochs
  carried large, **lyre-shaped** horns that curve outward and upward and then inward at the
  tips [[aurochs]] · [[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/020-lyre-shaped-horns|Narrative —
  Lyre-shaped horns]], whereas the practical test between aurochs and wisent is "the shape
  and size of the antlers [horns]"
  [[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/011-bison|Narrative — Bison]]. But heraldry deforms horns
  for design, not zoology: the earliest Moldavian seals and coins show the head with horns
  **curved outward** (*coarnele curbate în afară*), while 15th-century coins show them
  **curved inward** (*coarnele curbate în interior*) — a change of heraldic convention, not
  of species [[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.70, p.72. Once the
  horns are stylised, the one diagnostic feature is compromised.

- **Build.** The two animals are shaped differently. The aurochs was "generally taller at
  the shoulders with more elongated limbs suited for environments rich in vegetation,"
  while bison are "bulkier with a more robust body adapted to open grasslands"
  [[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/009-aurochs-bison|Narrative — Aurochs & Bison]] — the
  wisent's mass concentrated in heavy forequarters versus the aurochs's longer-legged,
  athletic frame. A **frontal head** — the standard form of this charge — shows almost none
  of this: no flank, no back-line, no leg proportions.

- **Scale.** The aurochs was genuinely large: bulls stood about **180 cm at the withers**,
  cows about 160 cm [[../../sources/felius-2014-cattle-history/text|Felius 2014]] p.9. So
  was the wisent. Sheer size does not separate them.

The decisive point for the heraldry is that the charge is, by design, uninformative about
species. Tiron notes that the figure was drawn "**frontal, usually without a neck**" and
accompanied by firmament pieces, lilies or crosses — and that "in spite of the variations
of representation (*bour*, *zimbru* or *taur* [bull])" it keeps these same basic heraldic
traits [[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.65. A frontal, neckless
head strips away precisely the build and profile cues that would tell an aurochs from a
bison.

## Where it stands

Scholarship has not resolved — and in one influential reading does not need to resolve — the
species of the beast.

- The **aurochs (*bour*)** identification is the default of the Romanian tradition and of
  the emblem's own name: the *cap de bour*, the animal of the founding legend, a real native
  species that was organised game in the region
  [[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.63, p.66;
  [[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/023-romanian-aurochs|Narrative — Romanian Aurochs]].

- The **wisent (*zimbru*)** reading is supported by contemporaries who described the actual
  seals as a *cap de zimbru*, and by the well-attested drift of later art toward the bison
  once the aurochs was extinct — a drift the book ties directly to the 1858 stamps
  [[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.65;
  [[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/002-symbol-of-moldavia|Narrative — Symbol of Moldavia]];
  [[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/011-bison|Narrative — Bison]].

- The **methodological** position, argued by Tiron, is that the charge is best treated as a
  neutral **cap de bovideu** ("bovid head"): the heraldic case for a Maramureș origin of the
  Moldavian emblem holds "*indiferent de specia … zimbru sau bour*," and even his own English
  abstract keeps the door open — "chases of the aurochs (or bison)," an "ox head"
  [[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.65, p.83.

This page does not adjudicate among them. The most defensible summary is that the **emblem
is by tradition and name an aurochs, that its depictions have repeatedly slid toward the
wisent — especially after 1627, when no living aurochs remained to copy — and that a frontal
heraldic head cannot, on its own, prove which**
[[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/002-symbol-of-moldavia|Narrative — Symbol of Moldavia]] ·
[[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.65.
