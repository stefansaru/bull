# Auxiliary sigillary stamps — district, village, telegraph and customs seals

Beside the quarantine class ([[quarantine-posts|K]]), Dragomir's **Part 2** gathers four small
classes of **administrative seals that also struck the mail** but were never postal
offices in the strict sense: the **district** seals (`SAD`), **village** seals (`SAS`),
**telegraph-office** seals (`ST`) and **customs** seals (`SAV`)
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photos 92, 95, 96,
103). They matter to postal history because their impressions turn up **on letters and on
circulated documents** — sometimes alongside a proper postal cancel of the philatelic
period — and because most of them carry the same **heraldic devices** (Moldavia's
*cap de bour*, the Wallachian *acvila cruciată*, and after 1859 the joined arms of the
United Principalities) that the post's own seals use. Per-class device tables are at
**[[../handstamps/SAD|SAD]]**, **[[../handstamps/SAS|SAS]]**, **[[../handstamps/ST|ST]]**
and **[[../handstamps/SAV|SAV]]**.

> **Taxonomy note.** The four class letters `SAD`, `SAS`, `ST`, `SAV` are **not** in the
> book's front *ABREVIERI* list (see [[../references/dragomir-stampilografie-1990]]);
> Dragomir introduces them only in the §2.2–§2.5 section headers. And two of the four are
> internally inconsistent about their own letter — the telegraph class is headed `ST` but
> coded `TL`, and the customs class is headed `SAV` but its devices are coded `SV` (see
> the inconsistency notes on each device page).

## §2.2 District seals (`SAD` — *Ștampile Sigilare Districtuale*)

The **first administrative handstamps found on letters**, from the period **1824–1831**,
all Moldavian: negative (sigillary) seals of the *ținut* (county) administrations
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 92). Dragomir
catalogues six — **Herța 1824, Hîrlău 1824, Vaslui 1827, Cîrligătura 1828, Dorohoi 1830,
Botoșani 1831** (Fig. 173–178, Planșa 11) — each showing the *ținut*'s own emblem: a
**balance** (Herța), **flags, trees and birds on a wheeled terrace** (Hîrlău), a
**beehive with swarming bees** (Vaslui), a **crayfish under a Maltese cross** (Dorohoi),
and at Botoșani a party-per-pale shield whose first quarter is the **bull's head with a
star between the horns** ([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]]
photos 92, 94–95). Full table at **[[../handstamps/SAD|SAD]]**.

## §2.3 Village seals (`SAS` — *Ștampile Sigilare Sătești*)

Negative seals of **village authorities** (schools, tax collectors — *școli, percepții*),
applied to letters those authorities sent; in the philatelic period they are found
**completed by a postal dispatch/arrival cancel**
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 95). **Five are
figured** (Fig. 179–183, Planșa 12) — **Haimanale, Toporu, Păucești, Țintăreni, Soveja** —
while **Satul Podoleni** is described but carries no figure ([[../../meta/errata|errata #10i]]);
three more are merely listed (Hârlești, Drăgănești, Zănești). Several carry
a **county emblem** in the field: the four-horse **cart** of Vâlcea (Păucești), the **black
goat** of Neamț (Podoleni), a **fish** for Dolj (Țintăreni)
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 95). Full table
at **[[../handstamps/SAS|SAS]]**.

## §2.4 Telegraph seals (`ST` — *Ștampile Telegrafice*; coded `TL`)

The seals of the **telegraph service**, whose first lines were built in **1854/55**; the
first telegraph-station chief was the Belgian sergeant **Cezar Librecht**, at Galați in
1854, and by the start of **1859 there were only 16 telegraph stations open for
correspondence** (11 in Moldavia, 5 in Muntenia), run by borrowed Austrian officials who
were replaced that year by natives under a Cuza decree
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 96). Dragomir
lists the lines (Galați–Iași, Iași–Bacău, Giurgiu–București, București–Focșani) and the
stations opened 1859–1865, then catalogues eleven **`TL`** seals (Fig. 185–191, Planșa
13). The **oldest known telegraph seal is `TL55` Galați (1855)**, a pavilion under a
closed crown over a shield with the **bull's head, star between the horns**, signed
Librecht; the **`TL59` Inspectoratul General** shows the cruciate crowned eagle bearing
the bull's head on its breast — the joined device of the **Principatele Unite**
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photos 98, 99). Full
table at **[[../handstamps/ST|ST]]**.

## §2.5 Customs seals (`SAV` — *Ștampile Vamale*; devices coded `SV`/`SAV`)

The **customs houses** (*vămi*, formerly *schele*), set on the frontier — and only
exceptionally in the interior (București, Iași) — whose official seals were struck **in
wax or as negative smoke/ink impressions on documents and letters**
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photos 103, 106).
Dragomir catalogues **72 offices** in three geographic groups: **23 Danube *schele*
(1830–1910)** from Turnu Severin to Sulina, **37 Carpathian *vămi*** from Vîrciorova to
Mihăileni, and **12 on the eastern line** (the Prut and the "conventional" line) from
Mamornița to Tatar-Bunar. The figured examples (Fig. 193–200, Planșa 14) — Dorna, Cubei,
Vîlcov, Bicaz, Soveja, Cornu Luncii, Tatar Bunar, Kilia — show the **United Principalities
arms** (bull's head with star + cruciate eagle on acolate shields under a crown), except
the **frontier-guard seal of Vîrciorova**, a border-battalion (*grăniceri*) device with a
Cyrillic "PUNCTUL VIRCIOROVA" legend
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photos 106, 107). Full
table at **[[../handstamps/SAV|SAV]]**.

## How they relate to the postal seals

These four classes sit **beside the office seals** (`SM`/`SV`), the road-post forms
([[franco-porto-handstamps|FM/FV]]) and the quarantine seals ([[quarantine-posts|K]]) in
the [[pre-stamp-postal-markings|pre-stamp world]] — administrative rather than strictly
postal, but reaching the mail because letters passed through counties, villages, telegraph
offices and customs houses. The `SAD`/`SAS` seals are the **earliest administrative marks
on Romanian letters (from 1824)**; the `ST` and `SAV` classes carry into the **United
Principalities and post-classic** period (to 1910 for customs), sharing the heraldry of
the 1858 *cap de bour* stamps ([[../references/dragomir-stampilografie-1990]];
[[moldavia-heraldry]]).
