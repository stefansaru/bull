# Quarantine posts (K) — the Danube sanitary cordon and the disinfection of mail

Along the lower Danube, where the Principalities faced the Ottoman south bank, ran a
**sanitary cordon** of quarantine stations (*carantine*) whose job was to stop plague
and cholera from crossing the river. Because **letters, like travellers and goods,
crossed at these stations**, each quarantine held its own **seal** and struck the
mail it disinfected — and Dragomir devotes **Part 2, "Ștampile de carantină («K»)"**,
of the *Ștampilografie* to these devices
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 58). They are
a class apart in the pre-stamp record: postal markings born of **public-health law**,
not of the post office proper. The per-device catalogue is at **[[../handstamps/K|K]]**.

## The cordon: from 1 January 1830

The system was laid down by the **Regulamentul Organic** and its **"Așezămîntul
carantinelor"** (Statute of Quarantines), the founding act of the "cordon of health"
running the length of the Danube **from Vîrciorova to Galați**. On **1 January 1830,
twelve quarantines** were established in Wallachia along the river
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photos 58, 71). With
the Danube thalweg now the border with the Ottoman states, the Romanian Danube
quarantines were **administered autonomously by each principality**, under a general
inspectorate seated in Bucharest that supervised the whole
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 58).

The **Regulamentul Organic** graded the stations, and the grades shifted over time
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 59):

- **Carantine "mari" (class I):** Brăila, Calafat, Giurgiu.
- **Carantine "mici" (class II):** Călărași, Cerneți, Turnu, Zimnicea.
- **Cantoane / oboare de schimb (class III, "exchange stations"):** Izvoarele, Bechet,
  Oltenița, Piua Petrii — with Izlaz appearing as a "*punct*" in some years.

By a journal of **28 November 1832** Calafat was reclassed to 2nd class and Călărași to
1st; and in the same year, after repeated petitions by the Craiova merchants, the
exchange station **Bechet became a 2nd-class quarantine**
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 59). Between
**1834 and 1852** three quarantines changed their seat (and one, Călărași, its name to
**Stîrbei**).

## What a quarantine did to a letter

A quarantine was a walled compound — at Cerneți, *bordeie* (dug-outs) roofed with
reed, with buildings, warehouses, a hospital, an inn (*ospătărie*) and an **afumătoare
(fumigation house)** — staffed by a director, a physician (*doftor*), a midwife
(*moașă*) and grave-diggers (*ciocli*)
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photos 59, 66).
People coming from infected country served **ten days** of quarantine; goods and mail
were **disinfected** and only then passed on.

For philately the decisive act is the **"curățire prin afumare"** — cleansing **by
fumigation (smoking)** — after which the **sanitary seal** was applied as the proof of
disinfection. Dragomir reproduces the certificate: the **certification of disinfection
"prin afumare la carantina Severin, 13 oct. 1838", struck with stamp `K38`**, on an
*adeverință* acknowledging Romanian correspondence received by the local authorities of
**Kladovo, Serbia** — a unique piece (col. 9)
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] Fig. 125, photo 64).
Ships arriving from the sea were held off the bank until inspected; **"toate trimiterile
poștale ce treceau prin porturile dunărene"** — all postal items passing through the
Danube ports — were subject to the seal, "*operația se atesta prin aplicarea sigiliului
sanitar*"
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photos 67–69). Mail
crossed the river by the **"poșta cu barca"** (the boat-post) between facing stations,
e.g. Cerneți and the Serbian Kladovo
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 59).

## Case studies (Wallachia, §2.1.1)

- **Cerneți / Turnu Severin.** *"Karantina Cerneți"* took shape in **1830** at the edge
  of **Schela-Cladovei** (~7 km SW of Cerneți), a 2nd-class quarantine with 14
  salaried staff, director the *serdar* Vasile Strîmbeanu, the Transylvanian physician
  *Tzing Carol*, a midwife and two grave-diggers; it worked in permanent liaison with
  the **Austrian quarantine Rușava–Supanek (Orșova–Jupalnic)** in the Banat and the
  **Serbian Kladovo**. A report (no. 192, 24 April 1837) records passengers arriving
  from Rușava after ten days of Austrian quarantine being taken over at the Vîrciorova
  customs and made to serve ten more days of Romanian quarantine at Cerneți. After the
  Turks burned Cerneți in 1828, **presidential Decree no. 79 of 22 April 1838** founded
  the new town of **Turnu Severin** on the site of ancient **Drobeta**; the **quarantine
  was its oldest building**, still standing (Fig. 122)
  ([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photos 59–62).
- **Izvoarele / Gruia, Piua Petrii / Gura Ialomiței.** Piua Petrii moved on **1 July
  1834** ~3 km north to **Gura Ialomiței** ("noul local din zid"), by the Vadu Oii
  crossing used by Transylvanian shepherds wintering their flocks in Dobrogea; with six
  staff under *pitarul* Dimitrie Masuridis it used its own seal **K34**, and **K38** from
  1838 ([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photos 61, 64).
- **Carantina Milcovu.** When cholera appeared in Moldavia in 1830 the Comitetul
  Carantinelor (Bucharest) set up (**11 June – 10 Nov 1831**) an **"interior, border
  quarantine"** on the **Milcov**, the boundary with Moldavia, in the Cerdacu forest by
  Wallachian Focșani, under **Dr. Charles Barozzi** — the **"Cordonul Milcovului"**
  ([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 65).

## Moldavia (§2.1.2): the Direcția Carantinei at Galați

On the Moldavian side the **Direcția Carantinei** at **Galați**, legislated by **art.
178 of the Regulamentul Organic**, coordinated between the Sanitary Committee and the
operational units of Galați and (from 1856) all the Moldavian Danube ports; its seat
was inside the **Carantina Țiglina**, under director Iancu Cozoni, vice-director
Iordache Capșa and a chief physician
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 66). It
oversaw the **Carantina Țiglina, the Galați port captaincy and the exchange stations**.
The **Carantina Galați (Galați I, "Țiglina", "Lazaretul")** was built from 1830 on the
Mavromolu monastery estate on the Țiglina hill; a separate **Galați-port service (Galați
II)** already worked before 1830. Beyond the Danube line, **temporary quarantines
("vremelnice")** were set on the **"Linia Prutului"** — at **Sculeni, Lipcani, Leova** —
where danger of infection arose, plus **"interior quarantines"**
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photos 66, 67). In
**1854 the Carantina Țiglina was abolished**, its buildings passing to the Ministry of
Public Works and only the isolation hospital remaining
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 67). All
postal items through the Danube ports were subordinate to the Direcția Carantinei, whose
sanitary seal certified their disinfection
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 69).

## The philatelic record: the "K" mark

Dragomir codes these devices **`K`** — the initial of **"Karantină"** in the
orthography of the day — followed by the last two digits of the year the seal entered
use and the station name (e.g. **`K30 CERNEȚI`** = the first, 1830, seal of the Cerneți
quarantine)
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 71). He counts
**70 known Romanian quarantine stamps, of which 6 are unencountered**
([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] photo 71). This much
enlarges the earlier record: **Racoviceanu (1962, pp. 123–129)** described only **six**
Wallachian quarantine stamps; Dragomir's own **1979** *Filatelia* study ("Primele hărți,
sigilii și ștampile românești ale poștelor și carantinelor") is the basis of the
expanded treatment.

The stations and their `K`-codes are laid out in Dragomir's **"TABELUL K"** — 21
quarantines down the Danube against the columns `K30`–`K59` — reproduced as a device
table at **[[../handstamps/K|K]]**. The founding **`K30` type BRĂILA** is a round 24 mm
stamp showing the **crowned, cross-bearing eagle in flight on a green terrace**, the sun
above as an eight-lobed rosette, **no year**, and the marginal legend in capitals
**"KARANTINA BRĂILA"**; with variants **K30a** (variants of K30), **K30b** (2nd-class
quarantines), **K30c** (exchange stations, *oboare de schimb*), **K30d** (the
Directorate) — the matrices with which all twelve Wallachian quarantines were furnished
in 1830 ([[../../sources/dragomir-stampilografie-1990/text|Ștampilografie]] Fig. 127,
photos 70–71).

## Why it belongs in this book

The quarantine posts tie the pre-stamp mail to the **sanitary history** of the lower
Danube: a letter's route could be interrupted, its paper fumigated, and its passage
certified by a sanitary seal, long before an adhesive stamp existed. Their marks are
scarce, sometimes unique (the `K38` Severin certificate is a *unicat*), and they
document the **cordon, the ports and the cross-border correspondence** that the 1858
post inherited. They sit beside the office seals (`SM`/`SV`) and the road-post forms
([[franco-porto-handstamps|FM/FV]]) in the [[pre-stamp-postal-markings|pre-stamp world]].
