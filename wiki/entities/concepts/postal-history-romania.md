# Postal history of the Principalities to 1858

The Moldavian *Cap de bour* ("Bull's Head") stamps of July 1858 did not appear in
a vacuum. They were the technical by-product of a reform that finally pulled the
Moldavian post out of a centuries-old system of horse-relays and private
tax-farming and turned it into a state service that franked letters in advance.
This page follows that road: how mail actually moved before stamps existed, how
the routes and tariffs of the 1850s were rebuilt, why foreign post offices
crowded the field, and how the 1858 reform — through the new **Directorate of
Posts (D.P.D.)** and the **Central Postal Office in Iași** — created the demand
for an adhesive postage stamp that the [[../places/atelia-timbrului|Atelia
Timbrului]] then manufactured. For the wider political frame — the Phanariote
regime, the Organic Regulation, the Crimean War and the road to Union — see
[[geopolitics-1800s]].

## 1. The pre-stamp post: relays, olacuri and diligences

At the beginning of the 19th century the post in Moldavia and Wallachia was a
**state service organised principally for couriers and official missions**, not
for the public. Its costs were borne by the lower classes, while nobles and
officials were exempt
([[../../narrative/03-xixth-century/003-1800-postal-services-moldavia-wallachia|Postal
Services 1800]]). The physical network was a chain of relay stations called
**menziluri**, where a tired team could be exchanged for fresh horses; each
station was run by a **căpitan de menzil** (captain of the relay)
([[../../narrative/03-xixth-century/003-1800-postal-services-moldavia-wallachia|Postal
Services 1800]]).

Two kinds of vehicle worked the roads: the **olac**, a small uncovered carriage
built for speed and used for fast courier runs or a handful of passengers, and
the **diligență** (stagecoach), a large covered coach for longer journeys, goods
and more passengers
([[../../narrative/03-xixth-century/003-1800-postal-services-moldavia-wallachia|Postal
Services 1800]]). Anyone authorised to travel "by post" carried a **podorojnă**,
a travel warrant naming the route, the traveller, the number of horses due, the
date and the stations along the way
([[../../narrative/03-xixth-century/003-1800-postal-services-moldavia-wallachia|Postal
Services 1800]]).

The internal rules of this relay system are preserved in the old princely
ordinances reproduced in the standard history: every *menzilhanea* (post house)
was to be stocked with horses, drivers (**surugii**) and provisions; a captain
at the frontier issued the *carte de menzil* (relay pass); named clerks kept the
*condică* (register) of who passed and what toll — the **ugerit** — they paid
([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]]). This was a
transport-and-toll apparatus for officials and travellers; the carriage of
ordinary **private letters** was a secondary, and expensive, add-on.

## 2. A farmed-out service

Crucially, the post was not run directly by the state but **leased to
entrepreneurs**. In Moldavia the service was let to contractors (*antreprenori*,
*contraccii*) under contracts running three to five years; these men charged for
carrying private correspondence **by weight and by distance**, and the fees were
often so high — and the transit so slow, sometimes several weeks — that most of
the population simply did not use the post
([[../../narrative/03-xixth-century/023-1852-single-postal-tariff-moldavia|Single
postal tariff 1852]]). Through the Russo-Turkish wars and the Napoleonic era the
concession changed hands repeatedly; the entrepreneur **Scufa**, who already
farmed the Wallachian post, came to hold both at once, so that in the last years
before 1814 "the postal service of both Principalities was found reunited under a
single management"
([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.137).

## 3. Routes and networks

By around 1840 both capitals sat at the centre of a mapped web of postal roads,
commemorated in the book by the seals of the **Postal Directorate of Wallachia**
and of Moldavia
([[../../narrative/03-xixth-century/014-1840-postal-routes-wallachia|Postal
Routes Wallachia 1840]];
[[../../narrative/03-xixth-century/015-1840-postal-routes-moldavia|Postal Routes
Moldavia 1840]]). The 1916 history lists the through-routes to neighbouring
states: **Iași** was tied to **Russia** by two roads — via Mohilev on the
Dniester and via Chișinău toward Kiev, Moscow and St Petersburg, and to Odessa —
and to **Austria** by a single road through Botoșani, Dorohoi and Cernăuți to
Lemberg, which in turn linked to Warsaw, Vienna, Prague and Brünn
([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.137–138).
**Bucharest** reached Austria through Transylvania — via Pitești, Craiova and the
Turnu Roșu or Orșova passes to Sibiu and on to Pesta and Vienna
([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.138).

Internally the network was thickened in the early 1850s. On **1 January 1852** the
round-trip routes **Mihăileni–Iași** and **Iași–Galați** were opened — proposed
and debated a year earlier in the Moldavian Divan by **Teodor Ghica** and
**Nicolae Milo** — improving links between the north and south of Moldavia
([[../../narrative/03-xixth-century/022-1852-new-postal-route-moldavia|New postal
route 1852]]). A rare surviving route map of the Danubian Principalities, drawn
in 1848 and revised in 1854 (most likely by an Austrian official during the
occupation), records the state of the network on the eve of reform
([[../../narrative/03-xixth-century/030-1854-postal-routes-moldavia-wallachia|Postal
Routes 1854]]).

## 4. The reforms of the 1850s

The decisive change of the decade came in **1852**, when Moldavia became one of
the first states anywhere to adopt a **single (uniform) postal tariff**: a letter
was now handed in at the office for **40 parale per sheet**, the sheet was no
longer weighed, and the same charge applied regardless of distance; that the fee
had been paid was shown by a **FRANCO** mark struck with the **PM52** handstamps
([[../../narrative/03-xixth-century/023-1852-single-postal-tariff-moldavia|Single
postal tariff 1852]]; ⨀ [[../postmarks/kiriac-figures|PM52]]). Moldavia was
divided into four route zones, each assigned to a postal contractor — **Nicolae
Mavrocordat, Costache Moruzi, Teodor Ghica** and **Nicolae Milo**
([[../../narrative/03-xixth-century/023-1852-single-postal-tariff-moldavia|Single
postal tariff 1852]]).

That same year the **"Directorate and Office of the Diligences"** was founded in
Iași. Contract 9805/1852 between the government and the postal entrepreneur laid
down that the enterprise would provide administration, staff, salaries and
coaches; that **diligences would leave Iași for both borders twice a week**,
carrying passengers together with official and private correspondence; and that
private letters were taxed under **Ordinance No. 511 of 23 February 1852**
([[../../narrative/03-xixth-century/024-1852-directorate-and-office-of-the-diligences-moldova|Directorate
of the Diligences 1852]]).

The human hinge of the system was the **căpitan de poștă** (post captain). From
January 1852 the regional offices outside Iași were managed by **sameși**
(district cashiers) in the prefectures, who dispatched correspondence through
subordinate post captains — e.g. Dimitrie Pelin at Târgu Neamț — or commissioners.
Although the captains were paid by the private contractors, they had to lodge a
financial guarantee and were officially recognised as **employees of the Postal
Directorate**; offices accepted only inland letters, which were registered,
sorted, bagged and handed to the captains against a signed receipt, each letter
struck with the town name and date and, if prepaid, a *franco* mark
([[../../narrative/03-xixth-century/025-1852-postal-captain-moldavia|Postal
captain 1852]]). The rate remained fixed at **half a sfanț (40 parale)** for a
single sheet regardless of distance or weight, rising by a sfanț for each
additional half-*litră*
([[../../narrative/03-xixth-century/025-1852-postal-captain-moldavia|Postal
captain 1852]]).

In **1857** Moldavia briefly reversed course, introducing (14 February 1857, Old
Style) a **tariff based on distance and weight**
([[../../narrative/03-xixth-century/046-1857-postal-tariff-based-on-distance-and-weight-moldavia|Distance-and-weight
tariff 1857]]) — the tariff that the 1858 stamps would be designed to serve, and
whose very complexity would doom it within months (see §7).

## 5. From private concession to state monopoly

The reform's institutional groundwork was laid in 1857. On **23 April 1857**
Kostache Bosie rented, from the entrepreneur **Theodor Ghica**, fourteen rooms in
houses opposite the Orphanotropy Institute in Iași for 300 galbeni a year — the
premises that would become the **Central Postal Office** after the 1858 reform
([[../../narrative/03-xixth-century/049-1857-new-postal-headquarters-iaşi-jassy|New
Postal Headquarters 1857]]; see [[../places/old-post-buildings]]).

Then, on **10 November 1857**, the Moldavian government bought back from Teodor
Ghica the right to operate the stagecoach service granted to him in 1852; all
carriages and equipment across the country became state property, and the state
undertook to pay 14,000 galbeni
([[../../narrative/03-xixth-century/050-1857-the-moldavian-government-acquires-teodor-ghicas-postal|Government
acquires Ghica's postal service 1857]]). On **12 November 1857** the
Administrative Council endorsed the transfer, arguing that "in all civilized
countries the transport of correspondence and valuables is carried out under
direct state control," and appointed a five-member commission to plan the new
state-run service. In just the weeks of direct administration between **14
November 1857 and 1 January 1858** the state took **39,625 lei and 20 parale**,
exceeding expectations and confirming the case for a monopoly
([[../../narrative/03-xixth-century/050-1857-the-moldavian-government-acquires-teodor-ghicas-postal|Government
acquires Ghica's postal service 1857]]).

## 6. Foreign post offices

The Moldavian and Wallachian posts never had a clear field: they competed on
their own soil with the **foreign postal services of neighbouring states**, above
all **Austria** and **Russia**
([[../../narrative/03-xixth-century/023-1852-single-postal-tariff-moldavia|Single
postal tariff 1852]]). These offices operated under treaties the Great Powers had
concluded with the Ottoman suzerain, and after 1859 they not only continued but
expanded — the Austrian service in particular multiplying its offices and drawing
to itself almost the whole exchange of foreign correspondence
([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.228). From 1861
successive Romanian governments pressed Austria and Russia to withdraw them, but
without result: the Powers insisted their offices rested on the Ottoman treaties,
while the Romanian side replied that the **Paris settlements of 1856 and 1858**
guaranteed the Principalities self-administration
([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.228). The
statesman **Ion Ghica**, foreign minister in 1863, recorded a consul taunting him
that Romania lacked men able to run such a service — to which he answered that a
people who had mastered the differential and integral calculus could certainly
learn to sort and forward letters
([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.228). The
Austrian post in the country was only wound up on **31 March 1869**
([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]]).

## 7. The 1858 postal reform, the D.P.D. and the Central Postal Office

The measures of 1857 crystallised into the **PROJECT FOR THE REFORM OF THE
POSTS**, "drawn up by the commission appointed by His Excellency the
Prince-Caimacam and approved by the Administrative Council of Moldavia, to take
effect from 1 May 1858" (M.F. No. 1760 of 22 February 1858; A.S.B. fond D.P.D.
file 1040/1858 ff.37–68)
([[../../narrative/04-1858/017-1858-postal-reform-iaşi-jassy|Postal Reform 1858]];
[[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974]] p.28). The
first Moldavian postage stamps were "planned in accordance with the norms
established by this reform"
([[../../narrative/04-1858/017-1858-postal-reform-iaşi-jassy|Postal Reform 1858]]).

On **1 May 1858** the reform created two new bodies in Iași. The **Directorate of
Posts (D.P.D.)**, subordinated to the Ministry of Finance, supervised all postal
operations — accounting, guidance and control of the services still performed by
entrepreneurs — and had two offices, one for Director **Vasile Grigoriu** and one
for the controllers under **Ioan Bogdan**. The **Central Postal Office** handled
mail dispatch and passenger service through four offices: accounting (**Neculai
Costin**), dispatch (**Costache Cerchez**), day-to-day operations (**Costache
Bogdan** and **Gheorghe Vamiș**) and a registry holding the archives. Both
institutions operated from the premises Theodor Ghica had rented the previous
year
([[../../narrative/04-1858/018-1858-dpd-and-central-postal-office-iaşi-jassy|D.P.D.
and Central Postal Office 1858]]). To professionalise the new state service the
government obtained from Austria two officials and two coach-drivers — the
Bukovina postal administrator **Johan von Manowarda**, the clerk **Leon Scliba**,
and drivers Szergegdi and Nadler — whose arrival the Austrian agency in Iași
confirmed on **31 August 1858**; that October a set of **75 "Service Instructions
for the Conductors of the Moldavian Post"** was put into force, and Manowarda
stayed on as postal instructor
([[../../narrative/04-1858/032-1858-postal-instructors-join-dpd-iaşi-jassy|Postal
Instructors join D.P.D. 1858]]).

### The tariff — and the stamps

The reform's operating rules were the **"Instructions for the Conveyance of
Letters"** and its **Tariff (Lit. A), adapted by weight and distance**, which set
charges payable either **franco** (by affixing stamps on posting) or as
**postage due** (in cash, on receipt)
([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974]] p.28). A
simple letter up to **2½ dramuri** (about 8 g) cost **27 parale** for the first
distance — up to **8 poște** (relay-stages) — and **54 parale** beyond it; a
registered letter with return receipt cost **81** and **108 parale** respectively.
Thus a letter from Iași to nearby Bacău, Botoșani or Dorohoi, or within Iași
itself, cost 27 parale, while farther destinations such as Adjud or Bârlad cost 54
([[../../narrative/04-1858/022-1858-1-established-rules-for-letters-moldavia|Established
rules for letters 1858]];
[[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974]] p.28). The
tariff was reckoned **in parale** under Turkish monetary influence, 40 parale
making one leu
([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974]] p.28) —
which fixed the four stamp values at 27, 54, 81 and 108 parale, the equivalents
in French centimes being 25, 50, 75 and 100
([[../../sources/botosani-cap-de-bour/text|Botoșani — Cap de bour]]).

It was to serve exactly this **franco** option that adhesive stamps were ordered.
On **1 May 1858** (No. 975) the D.P.D. asked the Ministry of Finance to have the
stamps manufactured, naming first the values 27, 54 and 81 parale and later 108;
on **26 May 1858** the Ministry decided they should be printed at the
[[../places/atelia-timbrului|Atelia Timbrului]] (Stamp Workshop) in Iași
([[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974]] p.28). The
result — the four **"Bull's Head" (Cap de bour)** values issued **15 July 1858**
and first sold at the Iași office on 22 July, valid only within Moldavia — is
treated as the [[../issues/issue-bull-1|first Bull's Head issue]]; the manufacture
itself belongs to the [[../places/atelia-timbrului|Atelia Timbrului]] page and is
not repeated here.

### The tariff that failed

The distance-and-weight tariff proved a mistake. It was awkward to calculate and
unpopular with a public that, as the sources put it, "generally did not take
distance into account"
([[../../narrative/04-1858/034-1858-decision-for-single-postal-tariff-moldavia|Decision
for single postal tariff 1858]];
[[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974]] p.32).
Already in **September 1858** it was decided to abandon the zonal scheme and
**return to a single tariff** from **1 November 1858** — a flat **40 parale (1
leu)** for a simple letter and **80 parale** for a registered one, whatever the
distance
([[../../sources/botosani-cap-de-bour/text|Botoșani — Cap de bour]];
[[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974]] p.32). The
first Bull's Heads were withdrawn on 31 October 1858 after barely three months in
use and replaced by a **second issue** matched to the new single tariff
([[../../sources/botosani-cap-de-bour/text|Botoșani — Cap de bour]];
[[../../sources/kiriac-1974/chapter-1-postmarks-1858.en|Kiriac 1974]] p.32).
The classic tariff experiment had, in effect, come full circle back to the
uniform 1852 principle — but this time with a **postage stamp** as its instrument.

## 8. Aftermath: toward a unified national post

The Moldavian reform was the template for what followed after the Union. With the
**unification of the posts** of Moldavia and Wallachia — decided by the Council of
Ministers on 17 July and enacted by Decree No. 527 of **23 July 1862** — the
Iași Directorate was dissolved into a single administration, the Wallachian
tariffs were extended to Moldavia, and common **"United Principalities"** stamps
bearing both the **Bull's Head** and the **Eagle** were issued for the whole
territory
([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.221). Decree No.
615 of **7 August 1862** then separated the letter-post from the diligence
service and confirmed the post as a state-run monopoly
([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.222). The state
system that the Bull's Heads had inaugurated in one small principality had become
the national post.
