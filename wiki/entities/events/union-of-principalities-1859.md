# 1859 — the Union of the Principalities (Cuza)

> [!summary]
> On **5/17 January 1859** the elective Assembly of Moldavia chose **Alexandru
> Ioan Cuza** as its ruler; three weeks later, on **24 January / 5 February 1859**,
> the Assembly of Wallachia elected *the same man*. Two thrones, one prince — a
> single act of will that fused Moldavia and Wallachia into a de-facto united
> state, the founding moment of modern Romania. It happened barely months after
> separate Moldavia had issued its **first postage stamps, the 1858 Bull's Heads**
> ([[../issues/issue-bull-1]]) — the emblem of a country that was, on that very
> threshold, about to stop existing on its own.

This page is an **event** in the geopolitical spine of the story — see
[[../concepts/geopolitics-1800s]] — and it is the reason the 1858 *cap de bour*
matters far beyond philately: those stamps are the first and almost the last
postal signature of Moldavia as a sovereign postal authority. On why that emblem
carries the weight of a nation, see [[../concepts/why-bull-heads-matter]] and the
founding legend at [[dragos-founding-moldova]].

## The door opens: the Treaty of Paris, 1856

The Union was made possible by the aftermath of the [[crimean-war-principalities|Crimean War]]
(1853–1856). On that fault-line the "Romanian question" moved to the front rank
of European diplomacy, becoming — in the phrase the historians quote — "a veritable
cornerstone of the European equilibrium" ([[../../sources/carol-i-patru-regi/text|Carol I history]] p.22).

By the **Treaty of Paris (18/30 March 1856)** the Romanian lands were placed under
the collective guarantee of the Great Powers. Its **article 24** obliged the
Ottoman Porte to convene at once, in *each* of the two Principalities, an **ad-hoc
divan** meant to "constitute the most exact representation of the interests of all
classes of society" and to voice the population's wishes on the "definitive
organization of the Principalities." A special commission of the signatory states
— Britain, France, Austria, Russia, Turkey, Prussia and Sardinia — was to weigh
those wishes and report back to Paris ([[../../sources/carol-i-patru-regi/text|Carol I history]] p.22).

The primary consular record makes plain how contested this door was. At the
Congress of Paris the Austrian plenipotentiaries "fiercely combated" the Union,
and the foreign minister **Buol** was hostile to it — in one English paper's
phrase — "to the point of exhaustion" ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.18).
Already on 6 July 1856 Buol was urging the Porte to *forbid* the future ad-hoc
divans from so much as discussing Union, since keeping the two countries apart
would preserve them "in a state of perpetual weakness"
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] pp.18–19). The
Austrian consulate's own dispatches, gathered in this collection, run from the
summer of 1856 — when, following the Congress of Paris, the reigning princes were
replaced by *caimacami* (regents) and the unionist movement swelled into a mass
current — down to the autumn of 1859, when the Great Powers finally recognized the
double election of Cuza ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.9).

## The ad-hoc Divans and the drive for union

The elections to the divans were won by the **unionists**. The ad-hoc assemblies,
meeting at **Iași and Bucharest**, voted on **7/19 and 9/21 October 1857**
respectively for the union of the two countries into a single state under a
**hereditary foreign prince** drawn from a European dynasty — the program that had
won a broad national consensus ([[../../sources/carol-i-patru-regi/text|Carol I history]] pp.22–23).

The Great Powers sensed that a foreign prince pointed toward detaching the
Principalities from Ottoman suzerainty and toward independence, and they **rejected**
that clause. The compromise, the **Convention of Paris (7/19 August 1858)**, kept
the Principalities under the suzerainty of the sultan and the collective guarantee
of the Powers, but gave each country a *domn* (ruler) and an Assembly, with a
Central Commission at Focșani for laws of common interest. The ruler was to be
**elected for life** by the Assembly (art. 10), and eligibility was hedged with
conditions — age 35, born of a native father, a substantial income, a decade of
public service ([[../../sources/carol-i-patru-regi/text|Carol I history]] p.23).
Crucially, the Convention was drafted for *two separate rulers* — it never imagined
one man holding both thrones.

## The double election of Cuza

**Moldavia — 5/17 January 1859.** The Iași contest was a three-way struggle between
the party of the ex-prince **Mihail Sturdza**, that of his son **Grigore Sturdza**,
and the **National (unionist) Party** ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.505).
Grigore Sturdza had gone as far as massing some 1,200 Polish mercenaries, planning
to seize Iași and, if need be, assassinate unionist leaders such as **Mihail
Kogălniceanu**; the plot was betrayed from within and the mercenaries disarmed
([[../../narrative/05-1859/003-1859-alexandru-ioan-cuza-iaşi-jassy]]). On the night
of 3 January the unionist leaders settled on **Alexandru Ioan Cuza**, the commander
of the Moldavian army, as their candidate — a choice whose success took the public
by surprise ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.505;
[[../../narrative/05-1859/003-1859-alexandru-ioan-cuza-iaşi-jassy]]). On 5/17 January
the Assembly voted Cuza in with **48 deputies** — Grigore Sturza and four supporters
of his father abstaining — and the metropolitan proclaimed the new prince, after
which Kogălniceanu delivered his famous address
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.505).

The election, the editors of the primary collection write, "brought the final
defeat of the separatists": the rout of both Mihai Sturza (the reaction's candidate)
and Grigore Sturza marked "the complete victory of the unionist party — separatism
was definitively broken" ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.54).
Kogălniceanu's words to Cuza fixed the meaning of the day: "By electing you ruler
in our country, we wished to show the world what the whole country desires: new
laws, a new man… Be, then, the man of the age; make the law replace arbitrary rule…
be a simple, good, citizen-prince" ([[../../sources/carol-i-patru-regi/text|Carol I history]] p.24).

**Wallachia — 24 January / 5 February 1859.** In Bucharest the conservatives had
actually *won* the Assembly, taking 46 of 72 seats
([[../../narrative/05-1859/004-1859-alexandru-ioan-cuza-bucureşti]]). But on the
night of 23–24 January the National Party leaders, meeting at the Hotel Concordia,
seized on the one legal escape the Convention left open: since it forbade nothing
against it, elect **the same man Moldavia had chosen**. In a secret session on
**24 January / 5 February 1859**, invoking "the spirit of the Paris Convention,"
the deputies voted **Alexandru Ioan Cuza** ruler of Wallachia with 64 votes
([[../../narrative/05-1859/004-1859-alexandru-ioan-cuza-bucureşti]]). The consular
record catches Vienna's alarm in real time: "the election at Bucharest increases
Austria's panic. On 5 February, Godel Lannoy announces to Buol that Alexandru Ioan
Cuza has accepted the throne of Wallachia" ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.59).

## The de-facto union — a *fait accompli*

Cuza's double election produced the **union of Moldavia and Wallachia in fact**,
achieved *without* formally breaching the letter of the Convention — the Romanians,
the historians conclude, had "imposed their will on the Guarantor Powers by electing
as ruler of both Moldavia and Wallachia one and the same person"
([[../../sources/carol-i-patru-regi/text|Carol I history]] p.24). Austria understood
exactly what had happened. On 6 February Buol telegraphed Vienna's man in
Constantinople that the double election had "strikingly crowned the manoeuvres of
the party of disorder and anarchy," and the next day urged the Porte to protest
"with all its might against the illegality of the accomplished fact" — which was
itself an admission that Austria now had to resign itself to a conference of the
Powers ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] pp.59–60).

Recognition came step by step. Cuza's first objective as ruler was to have the
Union accepted by the Great Powers; one after another — France, Russia, Britain,
Sardinia, Prussia, then Austria and the Ottoman Empire — recognized him as ruler
of the United Principalities for the term of his life
([[../../sources/carol-i-patru-regi/text|Carol I history]] p.25). The de-facto union
of 1859 was then hardened into a single state: on 11/23 December 1861 Cuza told the
inhabitants of the Principates, "Your elect gives you today a single Romania… the
Union is accomplished"; on 22 January 1862 the first single government was formed
under Barbu Catargiu, and on 24 January 1862 the joint Assembly opened, where Cuza
proclaimed the definitive union under the name of **Romania**, with its capital at
Bucharest ([[../../sources/carol-i-patru-regi/text|Carol I history]] p.25).

## Why it matters — and why the Bull's Heads matter

The 1859 double election is the hinge on which the whole story turns. Moldavia — the
small principality whose aurochs-head emblem the [[dragos-founding-moldova|founding
legend]] traces back to a 14th-century hunt — issued its **first postage stamps in
1858**, the four values of the first **Bull's Head** set (27, 54, 81 and 108 parale;
[[../issues/issue-bull-1]]), followed by the three values of the second issue (5, 40
and 80 parale; [[../issues/issue-bull-2]]). Those stamps were printed and used in the
**last months of separate Moldavia**, on the very eve of Union: a small state's first
and — under its own name and its own emblem — almost its last postal signature.

The archival timing is stark. The order to **destroy 12,308 unsold stamps of the
first *cap de bour* issue** was minuted on **5 March 1859** and confirmed on 10 March
1859 — that is, *weeks* after Cuza's double election had already erased separate
Moldavia's future ([[../../narrative/05-1859/006-1859-1-return-of-unused-stamps-iaşi-jassy]]).
The Bull's Heads thus bracket the vanishing of the state that issued them: the aurochs
of Moldavia made its debut on paper just as Moldavia was about to be absorbed into
Romania. That is precisely why these fragile scraps carry a national weight out of all
proportion to their size — the argument developed at [[../concepts/why-bull-heads-matter]].

## Chronology (Julian / Gregorian)

- **18/30 March 1856** — Treaty of Paris; ad-hoc divans mandated ([[../../sources/carol-i-patru-regi/text|Carol I history]] p.22).
- **7/19 & 9/21 October 1857** — ad-hoc assemblies of Iași and Bucharest vote for union under a foreign prince ([[../../sources/carol-i-patru-regi/text|Carol I history]] pp.22–23).
- **7/19 August 1858** — Convention of Paris ([[../../sources/carol-i-patru-regi/text|Carol I history]] p.23).
- **1858** — Moldavia's first postage stamps, the Bull's Heads ([[../issues/issue-bull-1]], [[../issues/issue-bull-2]]).
- **5/17 January 1859** — Cuza elected ruler of Moldavia, 48 votes ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] pp.54, 505).
- **24 January / 5 February 1859** — Cuza elected ruler of Wallachia, 64 votes; de-facto union ([[../../narrative/05-1859/004-1859-alexandru-ioan-cuza-bucureşti]]; [[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.59).
- **5–10 March 1859** — remaining first-issue Bull's Head stamps ordered destroyed ([[../../narrative/05-1859/006-1859-1-return-of-unused-stamps-iaşi-jassy]]).
- **1/13 April – autumn 1859** — the Great Powers recognize the double election ([[../../narrative/05-1859/004-1859-alexandru-ioan-cuza-bucureşti]]; [[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.9).
- **11/23 Dec 1861 – 24 Jan 1862** — Cuza proclaims the definitive union under the name Romania ([[../../sources/carol-i-patru-regi/text|Carol I history]] p.25).
