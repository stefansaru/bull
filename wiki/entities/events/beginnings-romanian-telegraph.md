# The beginnings of the Romanian telegraph

> [!summary]
> The electric telegraph reached the Principalities in the same mid-19th-century
> modernisation wave that produced the 1858 postal reform and the *Cap de bou*
> stamps. Wertheimer-Ghika devotes a whole part of his book to the subject —
> **Part IV, "Începuturile telegrafului român"** ("The beginnings of the Romanian
> telegraph") in his table of contents
> ([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 4) — but
> this volume contains **no such dedicated chapter**. (The scan is *complete*: the 99
> photos are 2-page spreads covering the whole book, front matter to back cover, with
> no missing pages — so the chapter is genuinely absent from this volume, not merely
> unphotographed. The five-part plan most likely spans the wider *Documentele Poștei
> Române* series rather than this single book.) What the pages *do* show, incidentally,
> is the telegraph already at work as a tool of the Moldavian post in **1858–1859**, the
> combined **"postal-and-telegraph station"** on 1862 letterheads, and the eventual
> **union of the Telegraph with the Post in 1864**.

> [!warning] A thin, context page
> This is a short context note, not a full account. This volume carries no dedicated
> telegraph chapter, so almost everything below is drawn from telegraph references
> *scattered through the stamp documents* rather than from a connected narrative. Claims
> are limited to what those fragments actually say; where the OCR is scrambled this is
> flagged.

## The telegraph in the book's plan

Wertheimer-Ghika conceived his *Documentele Poștei Române* as several parts. The
table of contents lists, after the two stamp "books" (*Cap de bou* and
*Principatele Unite*) and *Poșta pasagerilor* (the passenger post), a fourth part
titled **"IV. Începuturile telegrafului român"** — the beginnings of the Romanian
telegraph — followed by a fifth on the foreign post in the Principalities
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 4). The
telegraph therefore belonged, in the author's mind, to the same documentary story
as the [[../concepts/postal-history-romania|postal reform]] and the Bull's Heads.
The pages of that fourth part are, however, absent from the reproduced source.

## The telegraph already at work, 1858–1859

Even without the dedicated chapter, the stamp documents show that by **September
1858** — the very months the first *Cap de bou* stamps were in use — the Moldavian
**Directorate of Posts** and its district offices already communicated with one
another by **telegraphic dispatch** ("depeșă telegrafică"). A post referat of
September 1858 orders a matter to be settled "ca să telegrafuiască" ("so as to
telegraph it")
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 36); on
**24 September 1858** a district expedition reports to the Directorate "prin depeșă
telegrafică" (by telegraphic dispatch)
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photos 39, 41).
The usage continues through 1859: a Galați office notes doing a thing in advance
"spre a nu fi de nevoie a se cere prin depeșă telegrafică" ("so that it need not be
requested by telegraphic dispatch")
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 69,
17 February 1859), a Bârlad office likewise "a să cere prin telegraf" ("to request
it by telegraph")
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 74). The
telegraph was also serving government business across the Union: in a letter dated
**Iași, 20 January 1859**, the minister of foreign affairs recalls "Deputat ți-am
trimis o depeșă telegrafică prin care te invitam a primi să fii agentul Caimacamiei
în București" ("Deputy, I sent you a telegraphic dispatch inviting you to become the
agent of the *Caimacamie* in Bucharest")
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 8) —
evidence that a working line already tied Iași to Bucharest by early 1859.

## The combined postal-and-telegraph station

By **1862** the two services shared premises and forms. Among the documents on the
1862 changeover of stamps, one is printed on the letterhead **"STAȚIA POȘTALĂ ȘI
TELEGRAFICĂ"** ("Postal and Telegraph Station")
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 94), and
the administrative orders of that spring were themselves sent **"PRIN TELEGRAF"**
("by telegraph"), reproduced as a **"DEPEȘĂ TELEGRAFICĂ"** timed to the minute on
arrival at Iași
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photos 93, 95).
Post and telegraph were, by then, operationally intertwined.

## Union of the telegraph with the post, 1864

The source records the formal merger with a single dated fragment. Discussing the
types of Moldavian office listed in the 1856 *Manualul Administrativ* — *stații de
poște*, *birouri poștale* and *expediții poștale* — Wertheimer-Ghika notes that some
post stations also handled the transmission of correspondence and, after the **union
of the Telegraph with the Post in 1864** ("unirea Telegrafului cu Poșta ... în
1864"), of "corespondenței și telegramelor" ("correspondence and telegrams")
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 11). *(The
OCR of this passage is badly scrambled — a rotated page with lines out of order — so
the exact wording around the date is reconstructed; the year **1864** and the phrase
"unirea Telegrafului cu Poșta" are legible; DR-006.)*

That merger left an administrative trace the author himself relies on: for the dates
on which post offices opened, he cites the **"Darea de Seamă a Direcțiunii Generale a
Telegrafelor și Poștelor pe anii 1872/73"** — the report of the **General Directorate
of Telegraphs and Posts for 1872/73**
([[../../sources/wertheimer-ghika-1944/text|Wertheimer-Ghika 1944]] photo 46). By the
early 1870s, in other words, telegraph and post were run as one directorate — the
outcome the 1864 union had begun, and the reason a page on the post keeps meeting the
telegraph at every turn.

## Why it sits beside the Bull's Heads

The telegraph and the [[../issues/issue-bull-1|1858 Bull's Head]] stamps are two faces of one
moment: a small principality wiring itself into modern communications on the eve of
[[union-of-principalities-1859|Union]]. The [[../concepts/postal-history-romania|1858
postal reform]] created the state post that issued the stamps; within a few years the
same state absorbed the telegraph into it. This page marks that adjacency — no more,
because this volume carries no dedicated telegraph chapter (see the note above).
