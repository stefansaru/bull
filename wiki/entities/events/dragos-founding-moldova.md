# 🐃 The legend of Dragoș & the aurochs hunt

> [!legend]
> **This page tells a founding *legend*, not a documented event.** The story of
> Dragoș hunting the aurochs is the traditional origin myth of Moldavia — a
> *legendă cinegetică* (hunting legend) preserved in popular memory and later
> taken up by the written chronicles ([[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.76).
> It is retold below as a story. What historians can actually *document* is
> separated into its own section further down, per convention **C-17**
> (legend clearly labelled vs. documented fact).

The legend matters because it is the thread that ties the animal on the 1858
**Bull's Head** stamp back to the birth of a nation: in the founding tale, the
hunted aurochs becomes the emblem of Moldavia itself. See
[[../concepts/aurochs-symbolism]] and [[../concepts/aurochs]].

## The legend, as it is told

📅 Traditionally set in the **14th century**.

According to the legend, **Dragoș** — a lord from **Maramureș**, then in the
Kingdom of Hungary — crossed the Carpathians eastward on a great hunt. He and his
companions gave chase to a formidable **aurochs** (*bour*), and in a fierce
pursuit Dragoș struck the beast with several arrows
([[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/025-the-legend-of-dragoş]]).

During the chase his faithful hound, **Molda**, was gored by the enraged animal.
The wounded aurochs plunged into a river in a desperate bid to escape, and Molda,
though injured, leapt in after it — and **drowned** in the water. Dragoș pressed
on after the weakening beast, cornered it at last, and slew it
([[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/025-the-legend-of-dragoş]]).

Grieving for his fallen companion, Dragoș is said to have **named the river
Moldova after his dog Molda**, and the land around it took the same name — the
birth of a new principality. He resolved that the emblem of Moldova would be an
**aurochs head** ([[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/026-founding-moldova]]). In the
old chronicles the severed head was set up "for remembrance … on a stake on the
riverbank" (as **Miron Costin** recorded in 1677;
[[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.77).

The hunt that carried Dragoș from Maramureș to the Moldova River is celebrated in
the lore as his **descălecat** ("dismounting") — the moment marking the beginning
of Moldova as a political entity
([[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/026-founding-moldova]]). The aurochs head passed
into the arms of the country; on its symbolism see
[[../concepts/moldavia-heraldry]].

## Legend vs. documented history

> [!note]
> The retelling above is **tradition**. The paragraphs below flag what scholarship
> actually supports, and where the legend outruns the evidence. Where the two
> diverge, this section says so plainly.

### What historians document

- **The term *descălecat* is real and specific.** In the Romanian world it carries
  the political sense of *founding, creation, colonization, or settling* — the
  legend attaches this act to Dragoș's hunt, but the word itself denotes the
  institutional act of establishing a country
  ([[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.69).
- **A historical Dragoș existed.** A royal act of King Louis I of Hungary, dated
  **20 March 1360**, granted a *Dragoș of Giulești* six villages on the Mara
  valley for his part in the "re-settling of our country of Moldova." The old
  Moldavian chronicles place his coming into Moldova in **1359**, explicitly
  linking it to the legend of the aurochs hunt
  ([[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.70).
- **The founder's identity is itself debated.** Constantin Rezachevici proposed
  that *two* men named Dragoș were involved: an earlier founder who came around
  **1347** (an "*om nou*," a soldier raised by King Louis for military merit), and
  the later Dragoș of Giulești of the chronicle legend
  ([[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.70).
- **The aurochs head is a documented medieval heraldic charge in Maramureș** —
  older than the legend needs it to be. The earliest attestation of a *cap de
  bovideu* is on a **1383** document from Sighet; a **1421** seal bearing an
  aurochs head is attributed to a member of the **Drăgoșești** family
  ([[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] pp.64–65). Miron
  Costin already noted in 1677 that Sighet, the capital of Maramureș, bore an
  aurochs head as its seal, just like Moldova
  ([[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.63).
- **The most plausible scholarly explanation is borrowing, not the legend.** Tiron
  argues the aurochs head reached Moldova by being **carried over from Maramureș**
  heraldry, where the charge was already established, rather than being conjured at
  the moment of a single hunt
  ([[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.76).

### What is legendary (not established fact)

> [!legend]
> These specifics come from the *story*, and the sources treat them as legend, not
> record:
>
> - **The hound Molda drowning and giving the river its name.** This is the
>   folk-etymological heart of the tale
>   ([[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/025-the-legend-of-dragoş]]). Historically the
>   hydronym *Moldova* is instead traced to a Gothic root *mulda* (German *Moldau*,
>   cognate with the Vltava) — a linguistic, not a canine, origin
>   ([[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.63).
> - **Killing the aurochs = taking its emblem.** Tiron flags the underlying claim
>   as historically implausible: it would be the *only* case in all of medieval
>   heraldry in which the victor/founder identifies himself by the arms of the one
>   he defeated. He also cautions against the supposed "immemorial antiquity" of
>   the aurochs emblem east of the Carpathians
>   ([[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] pp.68–69).
> - **The exact circumstances of Moldova's founding.** No contemporary document
>   pins them down; the accounts vary, and the tale was likely *created* both to
>   fix a *terminus a quo* for the state's beginnings and to justify its historic
>   arms ([[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.76;
>   [[../../narrative/02-the-aurochs/026-founding-moldova]]).

### `debated` — was the hunted animal a *bour* or a *zimbru*?

The legend names the quarry an **aurochs** (*bour*, *Bos primigenius*), but the
Maramureș heraldic sources are deliberately non-committal: Tiron uses the neutral
*cap de bovideu* ("bovid head") precisely because, in his words, *"indiferent de
specia … zimbru sau bour"* — it is unclear whether the charge depicts the aurochs
or the **wisent / European bison** (*zimbru*, *Bison bonasus*)
([[../../sources/tiron-2020-stema-moldovei/text|Tiron 2020]] p.65). This identity
question is treated as an open **debate**, not settled here — see
[[../concepts/aurochs-vs-wisent]].
