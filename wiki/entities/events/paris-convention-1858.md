# 1858 — the Convention of Paris (the United Principalities' charter)

> [!summary]
> On **7/19 August 1858** the Ottoman Porte and the six guarantor Powers
> (France, Austria, Britain, Prussia, Russia, Sardinia) signed at Paris the
> **Convention** ("la Convention des Principautés du 7/19 août") that gave
> Moldavia and Wallachia a new constitutional order under the name **"the United
> Principalities"** (*Principatele Unite*) — a title Austria fought "with hatred
> and fury" ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]]
> pp.475, 19). It was a **deliberately partial union**: two separate princes, two governments,
> two assemblies, joined only by a common **Central Commission at Focșani**, a
> common Court of Cassation, and a unified army. Its own two-prince design was
> the trap the Romanians sprang six months later by electing **the same man,
> Alexandru Ioan Cuza**, in both countries ([[union-of-principalities-1859]]).
> The first **Bull's Head** stamps had gone on sale in Iași just weeks earlier,
> on 15 July 1858 ([[../issues/issue-bull-1]]) — so Moldavia issued its first
> postage while its very constitution was being redrafted in Paris that August.

This page is the **August-1858 hinge** of the geopolitical spine — see
[[../concepts/geopolitics-1800s]]. It sits between the [[ad-hoc-divans-1857|ad-hoc
Divans of 1857]] (which voted the national programme the Convention was meant to
answer) and the [[union-of-principalities-1859|double election of Cuza in 1859]]
(which turned the Convention against itself). For the regency government that
administered Moldavia under its terms, see [[../concepts/caimacam-regency-moldavia]].

## What the Convention decided

The Convention was the Powers' answer to the ad-hoc Divans, which in October 1857
had asked for a single united state under a hereditary foreign prince. The Powers
refused the foreign prince and refused a full merger, but conceded a framework
common enough to be, in the words of the Austrian negotiator Hübner, a Union "in
republican form" — which is exactly why Vienna fought it. Austria "combated with
hatred and fury the name *United Principalities*, and the common flag" (and, with
Britain, even pressed to plant a crescent on that common flag), and resisted every
clause enlarging the Central Commission, since a broadened Commission "would make
the Union a reality" ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.19).
The pro-union Powers, above all Russia, blunted the Austrian theses, yet the result
was still a hedged, minimal union: Kiselev could exclaim at the close of the
conference that "the two Principalities were less united now than at the moment the
[Organic] Regulations had been promulgated"
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.19).

**Two of everything.** The Convention kept the Principalities under the sultan's
suzerainty and the Powers' collective guarantee, and gave *each* country its own
**prince (hospodar), government and Assembly**. The primary text is explicit that
the electing Assembly "would elect **in each of the Principalities a hospodar**"
— *elirait dans chacune des Principautés un hospodar*
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.531). This
two-prince design is the single most important fact about the Convention: it never
imagined one man holding both thrones.

**A common Central Commission at Focșani.** The one genuinely unifying organ was a
**Central Commission** seated at **Focșani** (art. 27 of the Convention), charged
with harmonising the laws of common interest and with reconciling the two
Assemblies; a common Court of Cassation and a unified army completed the shared
scaffolding ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.476).

**One electoral law for both countries.** The Convention annexed a single electoral
statute — "the Convention being absolutely the same for Moldavia as for Wallachia"
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.476). It fixed
the franchise by **property census (art. 33)**, and required every prospective
elector "to justify his rights and his revenue" (**art. 8**)
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.533). The
census was, by the frank admission recorded in the archive, **"too conservative"**
— *le cens électoral posé par la Convention était trop conservateur* — which is why
the Moldavian regency stretched its interpretation to widen the unionist electorate
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.532).

**Abolition of boyar rank and privilege.** The Convention was drafted to enshrine
the levelling programme the Divans had already voted. This collection does **not**
reproduce that article of the Convention verbatim (see the source-gap flagged
below), but it does document the underlying vote: on 29 October / 10 November 1857
the Moldavian ad-hoc Divan adopted, by majority amendment, the principle "that
henceforth **all privileges of the several estates shall be abolished**"
(*dass künftighin alle Privilegien der einzelnen Stände aufgehoben werden sollen*),
to the fury of the aristocracy, alongside its reports on "equality before the law"
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.352).

## How the Convention took effect in Moldavia

The Convention was promulgated by an Ottoman **Hatt-i şerif**: "In execution of the
Convention of last 19 August, the Hatt-i şerif of His Imperial Majesty promulgating
the **new organisation of the Principality of Moldavia**, together with the imperial
firman on the formation of the new Caimacamie, have just been dispatched"
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.444). That
single sentence is the political frame around the *cap de bour*: in the very autumn
the first Bull's Heads were circulating, Moldavia's constitution was being replaced
from Paris.

Crucially, the Convention (art. 49) ordered that "the present caimacams shall be
replaced by an **interim Commission**… to proceed to the election of a General
Assembly which would itself elect, in each of the Principalities, a hospodar"
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.531). In
Moldavia this **caimacamie of three** ([[../concepts/caimacam-regency-moldavia]])
fell to a unionist majority, so that — as the collection's editors note — although
the Powers had *not* granted Union, "the Convention provided for the replacement of
the caimacams and **indirectly ensured the success of the cause of Union**"
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.40). The
metropolitan of Moldavia sat as "permanent president of the Assembly, by virtue of
the Convention of 7/19 August"
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.493).

## The two-prince trap and how it was sprung

The Convention's fatal gap was that it forbade nothing against electing the same
person twice. The Austrian consul's dossier catches the plan being laid in the open:
"The aim was **to elect the same hospodar in both Principalities!** If the Moldavian
elections took place before Wallachia's, the latter was to elect Moldavia's man, and
vice-versa" ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.535).

That is precisely what happened. On **5/17 January 1859** the Moldavian Assembly
proclaimed **Colonel Alexandru Ioan (Aleco) Cuza** hospodar of Moldavia
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.534) — an
election that, the editors write, "brought the final defeat of the separatists"
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.54). On
**24 January / 5 February 1859** Wallachia elected the same man, making Union "an
accomplished fact by the **double election of the same hospodar**," whereupon the
Assemblies moved to reunite the two chambers at **Focșani** and consummate the
Union ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.535). The
Romanians had used the letter of the Convention to defeat its intent — a *fait
accompli* the guarantor Powers were left to ratify. The full 1859 story is at
[[union-of-principalities-1859]].

> [!note]+ Dating (convention C-04)
> The Convention is dated **7/19 August 1858** — Julian 7 / Gregorian 19. The
> archive uses both styles interchangeably: "la Convention des Principautés du
> 7/19 août" (p.475) but "la Convention du 19 août dernier" (p.444). Cuza's two
> elections are likewise given Old Style / New Style: 5/17 January and 24 January /
> 5 February 1859.

## Why it matters for the Bull's Heads

The Convention makes the exact political weather of the 1858 stamps legible. The
first *cap de bour* issue ([[../issues/issue-bull-1]]) went on sale on 15 July 1858,
four weeks before the Convention was signed; it was used through the autumn under an
Ottoman Hatt-i şerif that was busy "promulgating the new organisation of the
Principality of Moldavia" (p.444). The stamps are therefore the postal signature of
a state whose constitution was being rewritten over its head — issued by a separate
Moldavia that the Convention had already scheduled for a shared future, and that
Cuza's double election would, within six months, fold into the country soon to be
called Romania. That collision of dates — a first stamp and a last constitution in
the same season — is a large part of why these fragile scraps carry a national
weight: see [[../concepts/why-bull-heads-matter]].

## Timeline (Julian / Gregorian)

- **7/19 & 9/21 October 1857** — ad-hoc Divans of Iași and Bucharest vote for union under a foreign prince; the Moldavian Divan also votes to abolish all estate privileges ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.352). See [[ad-hoc-divans-1857]].
- **15 July 1858** — Moldavia issues its first postage stamps, the Bull's Heads ([[../issues/issue-bull-1]]).
- **7/19 August 1858** — the **Convention of Paris** signed: two princes, two assemblies, a Central Commission at Focșani (art. 27), a common electoral law (arts. 8, 33) ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] pp.475–476, 531–533).
- **Autumn 1858** — the Ottoman Hatt-i şerif promulgates the "new organisation of the Principality of Moldavia"; the caimacam Vogoride is replaced by a caimacamie of three (art. 49) ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] pp.444, 40). See [[../concepts/caimacam-regency-moldavia]].
- **5/17 January 1859** — Moldavia proclaims Alexandru Ioan Cuza hospodar; "final defeat of the separatists" ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] pp.534, 54).
- **24 January / 5 February 1859** — Wallachia elects the same man; the "double election of the same hospodar" makes Union a *fait accompli* ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.535). See [[union-of-principalities-1859]].
