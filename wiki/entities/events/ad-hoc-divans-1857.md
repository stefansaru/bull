# 1857 — the ad-hoc Divans and the vote for Union

> [!summary]
> In 1857 the Great Powers let Moldavia and Wallachia speak for themselves. Under
> the **Treaty of Paris (18/30 March 1856)** the Ottoman Porte had to convene, in
> each Principality, a consultative **ad-hoc Divan** (*Divanul ad-hoc*) to voice
> the population's wish on their future organization. In Moldavia the vote was first
> **stolen**: caimacam **Nicolae Vogoride**, coached by the Austrian consulate,
> falsified the electoral lists and the July 1857 elections. The fraud triggered a
> European crisis; at the **Osborne meeting (August 1857)** Napoleon III and Queen
> Victoria struck a bargain that **annulled** the Moldavian elections and forced a
> re-run, which the **unionists won**. On **7/19 October 1857** the re-elected Divan
> at Iași adopted the five points demanding **union of the Principalities under a
> foreign hereditary prince, autonomy, and neutrality**. This is the political ground
> on which, months later, separate Moldavia printed its first stamps — the 1858
> **Bull's Heads** ([[../issues/issue-bull-1]]). The union itself came in 1859:
> [[union-of-principalities-1859]].

This page is the year *before* the 1858 *cap de bour* — the moment when a Moldavia
still ruled by an Ottoman-appointed regent openly voted itself out of existence and
into a united Romania. For the wider frame see [[../concepts/geopolitics-1800s]]; for
the precondition, the [[crimean-war-principalities|Crimean War]]; for the regency that
governed Moldavia through it all, [[../concepts/caimacam-regency-moldavia]].

## The mandate: the Treaty of Paris and the firman

The door was opened by the **Congress of Paris**, which closed the Crimean War in
March 1856 — the collection dates its decisive session to **18 March 1856** (Old Style;
30 March New Style) ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.26).
The Treaty of Paris obliged the Porte to convene an **ad-hoc Divan** in each Principality,
so that the two countries could state their wishes on their "definitive organization"
— the article-24 mandate whose text the sister page quotes ([[union-of-principalities-1859]]).

Austria fought the instrument from the first. As early as **6 July 1856** the foreign
minister **Buol** was already pressing the Porte to *forbid* the future ad-hoc Divans
from so much as discussing Union, and that autumn, "on the occasion of drafting the
firman convoking the divans," Vienna tried to smuggle into the act a limitation on the
assemblies' powers; on **14 October 1856**, under Austrian pressure, the Porte issued
a circular rejecting Union in principle
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.19). The **firman
of convocation** was nonetheless issued by the Porte at the **beginning of 1857**
("La începutul anului 1857, Poarta emite firmanul de convocare a Divanului ad-hoc"),
sharpening the struggle between unionists and separatists
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.35).

## Vogoride and the machinery of fraud

Moldavia was governed in this decisive year not by a prince but by a *caimacam*
(regent). When the separatist regent Teodor (Toderiță) Balș died on **1 March 1857**,
the office passed — after a short interregnum — to **Nicolae Conachi-Vogoride**,
son-in-law of the poet Costache Conachi. Though he had given the French consul a
written pledge, Vogoride "from the first days went over to the separatist camp,"
continuing his predecessor's treasonous line
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.35). Installed on
**18 March 1857**, he became "the executant of the monstrous work of falsification."
The Austrian consul **Godel Lannoy** played a central part: this was "not merely an
interference, but a direct organization of the separatist intrigues," run with Vogoride
and the interior minister **Costin Catargi**
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.36).

The fraud was worked, above all, on the **electoral lists** — the notorious "list"
manipulation. Through the spring the regime carried out what the consul euphemistically
called an "**épuration**" (purging) of the lists, timing the simultaneous publication of
the district lists so that unionists could not concentrate their "forces of agitation."
When the lists appeared they had simply **struck off** the leading unionists —
**Constantin Hurmuzaki, Petrache Mavrogheni, Dimitrie Ralet and Anastase Panu**
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.48). The
elections of **July 1857** were then "held under the known conditions of falsification"
and met with "a powerful wave of protests"; on **28 July** Vogoride asked the Porte to
authorise the opening of the Divan within eight days
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] pp.38, 49).

The unionists struck back in the most damaging way: they **seized and published part
of Vogoride's secret correspondence** — including an original letter from his brother
at the Ottoman embassy in London passing him confidential news and advice — laying the
manipulation bare ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.37).

## The crisis, Osborne, and the annulment

The falsification of the Moldavian elections "was resolved on the European plane." The
collection names **three essential moments** of the "crisis": the **rupture of relations**
of the four "unionist" Powers (France, Russia, Prussia, Sardinia) with the Porte; the
**Osborne meeting** — where, in the editors' words, "Napoleon III gives up full Union
but obtains the quashing of the elections" (*„unde Napoleon al III-lea renunţă la Unirea
deplină, dar obţine casarea alegerilor"*); and the **annulment of the electoral
operations** themselves ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.38).
The Osborne bargain of **August 1857** between Napoleon III and Queen Victoria is what
broke the deadlock: the elections were annulled and Vogoride was bound "to keep a perfect
neutrality in the new elections" ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.19).

The reaction had "overstepped the measure. The elections are annulled. The separatists
are defeated." This annulment, the editors write, "dealt a heavy blow to separatist
reaction," which knew that on an equal electoral footing it was beaten in advance
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] pp.49–50). The
annulment of the falsified July 1857 elections is presented as "the beginning of the
retreat" of Austrian influence, and the subsequent double election of Cuza as "the
victory of the unionist movement"
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.11).

## The re-run and the unionist victory

The re-run went overwhelmingly to the National (unionist) party. By **22 September 1857**
even Godel Lannoy was forced to acknowledge "the complete victory of the unionists in the
elections," and a month later he had to report the enthusiastic pro-Union demonstrations
and the festive illumination of "numerous" houses in Iași
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] pp.39, 43). Vogoride
himself, briefly reading the wind, now "favoured the unionists," dismissing his chief
accomplice in the first list fraud
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.50).

## The resolutions of the Divan (September–October 1857)

The Moldavian ad-hoc Divan opened in the autumn. At its inaugural Te Deum the monk-deputy
**Neofit Scriban** preached that the most sacred duty of the age was "to reunite all
Romanians in one state under a foreign hereditary prince, with the conservation of their
rights and autonomy" ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.298).

The assembly turned that into a formal programme. In its session of **7/19 October 1857**
the Divan adopted the **five unionist points** — reported the same week by Godel Lannoy
(dispatch no. 80) and passed "almost unanimously" after being read out by **Kogălniceanu**
and supported by **Hurmuzaki**, to ovations and a night of illuminations
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.324):

1. **Respect for the rights of the Principalities**, especially their **autonomy**, under
   their old capitulations with the Porte (dated in the document to 1393, 1460, 1513 and 1634).
2. **Union of the Principalities into a single state** under the name **Romania**
   (*„Vereinigung der Fürstentümer zu einem Staate unter dem Namen Rumänien"*).
3. A **foreign hereditary prince** chosen from one of the reigning dynasties of Europe,
   his descendants to be raised in the country's religion (and, per the debate, *not* from
   a neighbouring state's dynasty).
4. **Neutrality** of the territory of the Principalities.
5. **Legislative power** entrusted to a representative General Assembly.

All five were placed under the collective guarantee of the Powers who had signed the
Treaty of Paris ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.324).
The vote for Union in the Divan met the opposition of **only two deputies** — the
separatists **Alecu Balș** and the vicar-bishop **Hermeziu**
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.50). With the Divan's
work done, a **firman of dissolution** wound the assembly up, and the following year the
Powers answered its wishes only in part: the **Convention of Paris (1858)** withheld the
foreign prince and full Union but did provide for replacing the caimacami — a measure that,
in Moldavia, indirectly assured the unionist cause
([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.40).

> [!note] Calendar (C-04)
> These events are dated Old Style (Julian) in Romanian usage. The Austrian consular
> reports quoted here use New Style (Gregorian): Godel Lannoy's dispatch no. 80 gives the
> decisive Divan session as **19 October** (New Style) = **7 October** Old Style — hence
> **7/19 October 1857**. Single dates drawn from the consular reports (e.g. 22 September,
> 28 July) are New Style unless otherwise noted.

## Why it matters — the link to the Bull's Heads

The 1857 Divans are the reason the **1858 Bull's Heads** carry a national weight out of
all proportion to their size. The stamps were manufactured and used in Iași in exactly
this climate: a Moldavia governed by an **Ottoman-appointed caimacam**
([[../concepts/caimacam-regency-moldavia]]) yet, through its ad-hoc Divan, openly voting
to dissolve itself into a united Romania under a foreign prince. The four values of the
first *cap de bour* set (27, 54, 81 and 108 parale; [[../issues/issue-bull-1]]) are thus
the postal signature of a state that had, the year before, already declared it wished to
stop existing on its own. The political will voted in 1857 was executed in **1859**, when
the double election of **Alexandru Ioan Cuza** turned these resolutions into fact —
see [[union-of-principalities-1859]]. On the heritage argument, [[../concepts/why-bull-heads-matter]].

## Timeline

- **18/30 March 1856** — Congress/Treaty of Paris; ad-hoc Divans mandated ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.26; [[union-of-principalities-1859]]).
- **6 July 1856** — Buol presses the Porte to bar the Divans from discussing Union ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.19).
- **14 October 1856** — Porte circular rejecting Union in principle ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.19).
- **early 1857** — Porte issues the **firman of convocation** ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.35).
- **1 / 18 March 1857** — caimacam Balș dies; **Vogoride** installed as caimacam ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] pp.35–36).
- **spring 1857** — falsification of the electoral **lists**; Hurmuzaki, Mavrogheni, Ralet, Panu struck off ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.48).
- **July 1857** — falsified Moldavian elections; unionists publish Vogoride's seized correspondence ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] pp.37–38).
- **August 1857** — **Osborne** meeting; Napoleon III drops full Union but obtains the quashing of the elections ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] pp.19, 38).
- **September 1857** — elections **annulled** and re-run; **22 Sept** unionist victory acknowledged ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] pp.39, 49–50).
- **7/19 October 1857** — the Iași Divan adopts the **five points**: union, foreign prince, autonomy, neutrality; only two deputies opposed ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] pp.50, 324).
- **1858** — firman of dissolution; **Convention of Paris** withholds the foreign prince but replaces the caimacami; Moldavia issues the **Bull's Head** stamps ([[../../sources/unirea-principatelor-1959-vol2/text|Unirea docs]] p.40; [[../issues/issue-bull-1]]).
- **1859** — double election of **Cuza** executes the Divan's will ([[union-of-principalities-1859]]).
