# 1862 — unification of the Moldavian and Wallachian posts

> [!summary]
> Four years after separate Moldavia issued its first postage stamps — the 1858
> **Bull's Heads** ([[../issues/issue-bull-1]]) — the two Romanian posts were fused
> into one. On **17 July 1862** (Old Style) the **Council of Ministers**
> (*consiliul de miniștri*) decided the union of the posts; it was enacted by
> **Decree No. 527 of 23 July 1862**, which **dissolved the Iași Postal Directorate**
> from 1 August, extended the Wallachian tariffs to Moldavia, and ordered common
> **postage stamps for the whole territory of Romania** — stamps that carried, side
> by side, the **Eagle** (*Vulturul*) of Wallachia and the **aurochs head**
> (*capul de Zimbru* / *cap de bour*) of Moldavia. A second measure, **Decree No.
> 615 of 7 August 1862**, split the letter-post from the diligence service and kept
> the letters as a state-run service. The state postal system that the Bull's Heads
> had inaugurated in one small principality thus became the **national post** — and
> the joint stamps literally carried the Moldavian emblem into united Romania.

This page is the direct institutional sequel to the [[union-of-principalities-1859|1859
Union of the Principalities]] and to the 1858 postal reform described at
[[../concepts/postal-history-romania]] (§8 there is the short version; this is the
full account).

## From two directorates to one

After Cuza's double election the united countries kept, for a time, separate
legislative and administrative organisations — and therefore separate posts, "the
one at Iași led by V. Grigoriu and the one at Bucharest by Tănase Bolintineanu"
([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.211). The two
services would run in parallel "until **1 August 1862, the date of the union and
merger of their posts into a single general directorate, with its seat in
Bucharest**" (*data unirii și contopirii poștelor lor în o singură direcțiune
generală, cu reședința în București*)
([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.211).

The idea had been in the air since the Union itself. Already on **5 November 1859**
the finance minister of Moldavia sent his Wallachian counterpart a project to
organise the postal service "throughout the whole extent of the **United
Principalities**" (*în tot cuprinsul Principatelor Unite*), proposing a single law,
a unified joining of the postal lines of both countries, and one General Directorate
([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.219). By 1862 the
two organisations had become "almost identical," and the prevailing spirit of the
moment — to bring about the complete union of the Romanian lands as soon as possible
— "imposed the **merger of both postal Administrations into a single directorate,
with its seat in Bucharest**" (*contopirea ambelor Administrațiuni poștale într'o
singură direcțiune, cu sediul în București*)
([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.220).

## Decree No. 527 of 23 July 1862

"The decision to unite the posts was taken by the Council of Ministers in its
sitting of **17 July** and was approved by **Decree No. 527 of 23 July 1862**"
([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.221). It laid down:

- **The dissolution of the Iași Postal Directorate** from **1 August**, part of its
  staff passing to Bucharest and part to the Iași post office; among those transferred
  to Bucharest was the Austrian instructor **Iohan von Manowarda**, kept on in that
  capacity until October 1864
  ([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.221).
- **Panaiot Sevescu**, who had directed the Wallachian post since December 1860, was
  retained as director of the united posts
  ([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.221).
- **With the union of the posts, the Wallachian tariffs became binding on Moldavia
  too**, and new stamps were issued "with which to prepay correspondence **for the
  whole territory of Romania**" (*se emise timbre poștale... pentru întreg teritoriul
  României*)
  ([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.221).

## The joint stamps — Eagle and Bull's Head

The new stamps were the visible face of the union. They were rectangular with the
corners slightly cut, giving them the form of an irregular octagon; "**in the middle
of the stamps stood the ‘Eagle' and the ‘aurochs head', the emblem of both
countries**" (*«Vulturul» și «capul de Zimbru», emblema ambelor țări*), with a
**post horn** below, the value in *parale* above and below, and the inscription
**"Franco-Scrisorii"** at the sides
([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.221). This is the
issue collectors know as the **"United Principalities" (Principatele Unite)** set —
though note that the source describes only these design elements and does not record
a literal "Principatele Unite" legend on the stamps themselves (see debate below).

There were three values, **3, 6 and 30 parale**, used as follows: the **3 parale**
for printed sheets and journals; the **6 parale** for local (*loco*) letters, one
stamp per 10 *dramuri* of weight; and the **30 parale** for letters circulating
throughout both countries
([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.221). At first the
stamps were made by pressing the clichés **by hand**, until a tender was published
for a special printing press
([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.221).

The significance is hard to overstate. Moldavia's aurochs head — the *cap de bour*
that had appeared on the [[../issues/issue-bull-1|first Bull's Heads]] of 1858, on the
very threshold of the country's absorption into Romania — was now paired with the
Wallachian eagle and carried across the whole national territory. On why that emblem
carries such weight, see [[../concepts/why-bull-heads-matter]] and
[[../concepts/moldavia-heraldry]].

## Decree No. 615 of 7 August 1862

The service, "organised and operated on the State's behalf" (*exploatarea sa pe seama
Statului*) in great haste, showed defects — too many offices run by district cashiers,
too few runs, heavy diligences on impracticable roads. Fresh reforms proposed by the
finance minister **Al. Cantacuzino** were approved by the Council of Ministers and
confirmed by the ruler in **Decree No. 615 of 7 August 1862**
([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.222). Its first
points were:

1. **The letter-post was to be separated at once from the diligence service**
   (*Serviciul poștei de scrisori se va osebi de îndată de acela al diligențelor*),
   with 30 light postal carts bought to carry only conductors and the express mail
   throughout the country
   ([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.222).
2. **The passenger-diligence service was to be lifted from the State** and ceded to
   private entrepreneurs with some government aid
   ([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.222).

The letters thus remained a state service while passenger transport was handed back
to private operators — the pattern that made the carriage of correspondence a
state-run affair, as it had first become in Moldavia in 1857.

## Why it matters

The 1858 reform (see [[../concepts/postal-history-romania]]) had turned the Moldavian
post from a farmed-out horse-relay service into a state one that franked letters in
advance, and produced the Bull's Head stamps as its instrument. Four years later, in
1862, that model became the template for the **whole national post**: one directorate
in Bucharest, one tariff, one state-run letter service, and one set of stamps for all
of Romania. And because those stamps bore the aurochs head beside the eagle, the small
principality's emblem — first postal signature of a country that had almost ceased to
exist under its own name — outlived Moldavia to become part of the iconography of
united Romania.

## Timeline (Old Style; New Style = +12 days)

- **5 Nov 1859** — Moldavia's finance minister proposes organising the post across the
  "United Principalities" with a single directorate ([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.219).
- **17 July 1862** — the **Council of Ministers** decides the union of the posts ([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.221).
- **23 July 1862** — **Decree No. 527**: merger of the two administrations, dissolution of the Iași Directorate (from 1 Aug), Wallachian tariffs extended to Moldavia, common stamps ordered ([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.221).
- **1 August 1862** — the date the union takes effect; single General Directorate seated in Bucharest ([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.211).
- **7 August 1862** — **Decree No. 615** (min. Al. Cantacuzino): letter-post separated from the diligence service; passenger diligences ceded to private operators ([[../../sources/istoria-postei-1916/text|Istoria Poștei 1916]] p.222).

## Debates

> [!note]- Is "state monopoly" the right word for Decree 615?
> The prompt (and §8 of [[../concepts/postal-history-romania]]) glosses Decree No. 615
> as "confirming the post as a state monopoly." The source at p.222 does **not** use
> the word *monopol* in this passage; it speaks of the service being "operated on the
> State's behalf" (*pe seama Statului*) and, crucially, cedes the **passenger**
> diligence service to **private** entrepreneurs. So the state-monopoly claim holds
> only for the **letter-post**, which remained state-run — not for coach passenger
> transport, which was privatised by the very same decree (DR-006).

> [!note]- "Principatele Unite" as a stamp legend
> Collectors call the 1862 issue the "United Principalities" (Principatele Unite)
> stamps. Minescu (p.221) describes the design — Eagle and aurochs head, post horn,
> value in *parale*, "Franco-Scrisorii" — but records **no** "Principatele Unite"
> inscription on the stamps themselves. The name here is used in its conventional
> philatelic sense; the source does not attest it as an on-stamp legend.
