## 1859 📅 Grigore Sturdza 🌍 Iaşi (Jassy)

<!-- u:u00687 kind:quad -->
The Rival
The union of Moldavia with Wallachia in 1859, known as the "Little Union," was a turning point in Romanian history, marked by political tensions, plots and foreign intervention. The favorable context was created by the temporary weakening of Russia after its defeat in the Crimean War (1853-1856), which limited the Tsarist Empire's ability to intervene directly. However, Russia, Austria and the Ottoman Empire continued to try to sabotage the union, using political influence, agents and funding to control the Principalities. In this climate, the Unionist party had to resort to pragmatic strategies, sometimes even less than honorable methods, to achieve its goal.
A significant threat to the union project was Grigore Sturdza, son of former ruler Mihail Sturdza. He was backed by Russia and funding from Jewish bankers such as Shmul Rabinovich. His plan was to bribe politicians to secure the throne of Moldavia. If the peaceful method failed, Sturdza intended to seize power through a military coup, backed by a force of over 1200 Polish mercenaries. These troops were prepared to occupy Iasi, eliminate the unionist leaders and march to Bucharest to take control of both principalities.
