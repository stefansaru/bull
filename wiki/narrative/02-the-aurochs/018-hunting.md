## 🐃 Hunting

<!-- u:u00102 kind:text -->
📅 ~ 53 BCE 🌍 Rhine River

<!-- u:u00103 kind:quad -->
Hunting
Julius Caesar himself was intrigued by the „urus” (plural „uri”), as he describes the aurochs from a german forest in Book VI of his ‘Gallic Wars’.
Julius Caesar's description presents the aurochs as massive creatures, powerful and fast, which did not spare neither man nor beast once sighted. According to Caesar, aurochs were smaller than the now-extinct North African Elephants and resembled black bulls in appearance.  Caesar also noted the difficulty in domesticating these mighty creatures, which couldn’t form a bond with the farmrs even when the calfs were very young.
These „uri” were a popular choice among hunters because they provided plenty of meat. Hunting the aurochs however, was no easy task. Hunters had to use different strategies involving arrows, traps, and even hunting dogs to catch these powerful creatures. „Uri” were in the end, trapped using deep pits and then killed. The capture and defeat of such formidable animals were seen as a rite of passage for young warriors, preparing them for battle. Those who succeeded in killing these beasts were highly honored, bringing the horns as proof of their achievement.
