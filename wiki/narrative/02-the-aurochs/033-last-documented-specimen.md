🐃 Last documented specimen
In the quiet expanse of Poland's Jaktorów Forest, the last female aurochs met her end by natural causes in 1627. Her death marked the close of a chapter that had stretched unbroken since prehistoric times.
While the last recorded aurochs is traditionally said to have died in the Jaktorów Forest in 1627, the exact date of the extinction of the species is not precisely documented, leaving some room for the possibility that aurochs may have lived on undetected in more remote areas. 
The dense forests and numerous water streams of regions like Moldavia (Moldova) and Wallachia (Ţara Românească) would have provided an ideal habitat for these animals, potentially supporting small, unrecorded populations beyond the known extinction date.
Some modern cattle breeds exhibit features reminiscent of the aurochs, such as the light color stripe along the back of bulls, the lighter colour of cows, or an aurochs-like horn shape.
