## 🐃 Lydian Aurochs

<!-- u:u00092 kind:quad -->
🐃 Lydian Aurochs
Aurochs were significant in many ancient cultures, including those in the Near East and Anatolia. They often symbolized strength and virility. The Lydians, who were heavily influenced by neighboring cultures, likely adopted and adapted similar symbols. The bull was a common symbol of strength and fertility across many ancient cultures. Given the prominence of bulls in other contemporary cultures' art and mythology (such as the Minoans, Hittites, and Mesopotamians), it is plausible that the Lydians held the aurochs in similar regard. The physical characteristics of the bull on the coins—large size, muscular build, and prominent horns—align more closely with the wild aurochs than with domesticated cattle, reinforcing the idea that the aurochs was a symbolic animal for the Lydians.
