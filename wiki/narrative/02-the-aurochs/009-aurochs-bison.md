## 🐃 Aurochs & 🦬 Bison

<!-- u:u00059 kind:quad -->
🐃 Aurochs and 🦬 Bison (Wisent)
The aurochs and the bison, although distinct species, share several physical characteristics that often lead to confusions between the two. Both were massive herbivores with imposing horns and a strong physical presence in their respective habitats. The bison, comprising two primary species—the American bison (Bison bison) and the European bison or wisent (Bison bonasus)—populated vast tracts of North America and Europe, respectively. 
While the aurochs were generally taller at the shoulders with more elongated limbs suited for environments rich in vegetation, bison are bulkier with a more robust body adapted to open grasslands.  
The European bison, also known as the wisent, primarily inhabits broad-leaved or mixed forests within a mosaic-like landscape that includes open areas like meadows, clearings, and young plantations. These environments provide an ideal mix of cover and foraging opportunities. 
Coincidentally, both "aurochs" and "bison" serve as both singular and plural forms of their respective nouns, which is a linguistic feature not commonly found in English.
