## 🐃 Ice Age Megafauna

<!-- u:u00035 kind:quad -->
📅 Last Glacial Period 🌍 Europe and Asia 
🐃 Ice Age Megafauna
The Eurasian aurochs, known scientifically as Bos primigenius primigenius, roamed the forests and wetlands of Europe and parts of Asia and were one of the ancestors of modern domestic cattle. During the last Ice Age, the cold landscape hosted a spectacular array of megafauna. These were the giants of their time, creatures of immense size and fascinating adaptations perfectly suited to the cold world they lived in, with thick fur and big bodies that helped them stay warm and store energy.
Just as we can trace our ancestry back to those people who lived during the ice age, today's cattle can do the same, their lineage leading back to the aurochs. The wild aurochs however, were larger than today's domestic cattle. Proportionally, the aurochs had longer and more slender limbs than those of domestic cattle, their physical constitution was more athletic and muscular, and the horns were noticeably different.
