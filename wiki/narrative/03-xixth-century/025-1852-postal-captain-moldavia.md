## 1852 📅 📯 Postal captain 🌍 Moldavia

<!-- u:u00335 kind:quad -->
Postal captain
Starting in January 1852, regional post offices outside Iași were managed by sameși (district cashiers) working in the prefectures of regional capitals. They oversaw the dispatch of correspondence through subordinates such as post captains (e.g., Dimitrie Pelin in Târgu Neamț) or commissioners (e.g., Ioan Ghizdavăț in Adjud). Although post captains were financially supported by private contractors, they had to deposit a financial guarantee and were officially recognized as employees of the Postal Directorate.
Post offices were required to accept only letters addressed within the country. After registration, the letters were sorted by destination, packaged, and handed over to post captains, with a signed receipt.
Couriers received a dispatch sheet listing the date and the packages. Upon arrival, the receiving post captains confirmed delivery by signing and recording the time of arrival. These sheets were stored in the archives of the final office.
The packages were then sent to local post offices, where the letters were opened, registered, and distributed to mail carriers. Each letter had to be stamped with the name of the city and the date of submission. If postage was prepaid, an additional stamp marked “franco” was applied.
Postal rates were fixed: a letter written on a single sheet of paper cost half a sfanț (40 parale), regardless of distance or weight. Letters weighing between half a litră (162 grams) and one litră (323 grams) were charged one sfanț. For each additional half-litră, another sfanț was added.
Litră was a historical unit of weight or volume, roughly equivalent to one-third of a liter or kilogram.
