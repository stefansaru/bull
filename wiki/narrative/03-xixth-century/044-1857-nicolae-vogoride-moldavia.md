## 1857 📅 Nicolae Vogoride 🌍 Moldavia

<!-- u:u00434 kind:text -->
1857-03 📅 1857-10

<!-- u:u00435 kind:quad -->
Though Balș wielded considerable influence against the union of the principalities during his brief rule, his sudden death in 1857 threw Moldavia into uncertainty, paving the way for Nicolae Vogoride, another controversial figure with close ties to the Ottoman rulers of the Phanariot system. Vogoride’s ambitions were heavily influenced by promises from his Phanariot relatives, who assured him he could maintain control of Moldavia if he successfully obstructed the unification of the Romanian principalities. A key figure in undermining Vogoride's plans was his own wife, Cocuța Conachi. Forced to marry Vogoride, Cocuța turned against her husband, exposing his fraudulent activities during the Moldovan elections. Vogoride had rigged the elections to ensure a result that would maintain the division between Moldavia and Wallachia, thus securing his own political power. Cocuța courageously leaked the incriminating correspondence between her husband and his Phanariot connections, revealing the extent of her husband's electoral fraud. The documents were published in a prestigious Western newspaper, triggering an international diplomatic scandal that reverberated across Europe.
