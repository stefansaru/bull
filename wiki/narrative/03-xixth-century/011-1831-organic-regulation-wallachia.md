## 1831 📅 Organic Regulation 🌍 Wallachia

<!-- u:u00266 kind:quad -->
The Organic Regulation
In 1831 and 1832, the Organic Regulation was introduced in Wallachia and Moldavia, respectively, marking the first constitutional framework for these principalities. This regulation brought several pros and cons from the perspective of the local Romanian population. On the positive side, the Organic Regulation modernized the administration, established a clearer legal framework, and laid the groundwork for future reforms. It introduced a more structured government, improved tax collection, and provided some guarantees for property rights, which benefited the emerging middle class and landowners. However, there were also significant drawbacks. The regulations were imposed by the Russian authorities and were seen by many as an extension of Russian control, limiting genuine autonomy. The new system often favored the boyars (nobility) and maintained many of the existing social hierarchies, which marginalized peasants and lower classes. Additionally, the economic burdens imposed during the Russian occupation, coupled with the exploitative practices of the occupiers, continued to strain the local population. While the Organic Regulation laid the foundation for modernization, it did so within a framework that reinforced existing inequalities and foreign influence.
