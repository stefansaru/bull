## 1857 📅 First Russian Postage Stamp 🌍 Russian Empire

<!-- u:u00458 kind:quad -->
Third Rome
In the 15th–16th centuries, the idea of Moscow as the “Third Rome” emerged—a concept envisioning the new Russian capital as the rightful successor of Christian civilization after Rome and Constantinople. This vision was solidified during the reign of Grand Duke Ivan III, who, through his marriage to a Byzantine princess, reaffirmed both the dynastic and symbolic continuity with the Byzantine Empire.
Following the fall of Constantinople to the Ottoman Empire in 1453, Ivan III claimed the spiritual and political legacy of Byzantium, promoting Moscow as the new center of Orthodox Christianity. To reinforce this imperial legitimacy, he adopted the double-headed eagle—a traditional Byzantine emblem—as the official symbol of the Muscovite state. The two heads of the eagle represented Rome and Constantinople, emphasizing Russia’s dual inheritance and its aspiration to continue the imperial and religious mission of the two former capitals.
The first Russian postage stamp was officially issued on December 22, 1857. A postal order stated that, starting January 1, 1858, all private letters within the Russian Empire, including the Kingdom of Poland and the Grand Duchy of Finland, had to be sent with a stamp based on the letter's weight.
