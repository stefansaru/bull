## 1857 📅 📯 The Moldavian Government acquires Teodor Ghica’s postal service 🌍 Iaşi (Jassy)

<!-- u:u00455 kind:quad -->
The Moldavian Government acquires Teodor Ghica’s postal service
On November 10, 1857, the government of Moldova concluded an agreement with entrepreneur Teodor Ghica, a member of the same family as the former ruler. Through this agreement, the State repurchased the right to operate the stagecoach service, originally granted to Ghica in 1852. He relinquished all claims against the State, and all carriages and equipment used in the business, both in Iași and throughout the country, became the property of the State.
In exchange for this renunciation, the government committed to pay the entrepreneur 14,000 gold coins, of which 1,000 would be paid immediately and the remainder within one year, with 7% interest.
On November 12, 1857, the Administrative Council of Moldova reviewed the agreement between the government and entrepreneur Teodor Ghica regarding the repurchase of the stagecoach service. The Council emphasized that in all civilized countries, the transport of correspondence and valuables is carried out under direct state control, as such a system provides greater safety and guarantees than private operations. Therefore, it was considered appropriate for the Moldavian state to take over the stagecoach service.
To carefully examine this transition, a special commission of five members was proposed, including representatives from the central administration and the Department of Public Works. The commission was tasked with developing a concrete plan for the organization and operation of the new state-run service, covering both passenger transport and postal deliveries. The Caimacam of Moldova approved the proposal and entrusted the commission with integrating the future postal enterprise, whose contract was soon to expire.
Following the termination of the contract with Ghica, the Moldavian state established a monopoly on postal transportation. In just a few weeks of direct administration—from November 14, 1857, to January 1, 1858—the state generated revenues of 39,625 lei and 20 parale, exceeding expectations. This outcome demonstrated not only the economic efficiency of the takeover but also the advantages of enhanced control and security in the postal system.
In conclusion, the state’s acquisition of the stagecoach service marked an important step toward modernizing and centralizing Moldova’s communication infrastructure. The measure was justified both by financial benefits and by the need to provide a secure, efficient, and reliable public service.
