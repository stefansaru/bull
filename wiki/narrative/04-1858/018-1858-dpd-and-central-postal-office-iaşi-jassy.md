D.P.D. and Central Postal Office
On May 1, 1858, the Postal Directorate (D.P.D.) and Central Postal Office were established in Iași as part of the Postal Reform Project. The D.P.D., subordinated to the Ministry of Finance, supervised all postal operations, including accounting, guidance and control of postal services provided by entrepreneurs.
The Central Postal Office handled mail dispatch and passenger services, and both institutions operated from the headquarters rented the previous year by Theodor Ghica.
The D.P.D. had two offices: one for Director Vasile Grigoriu and one for the controllers, led by Ioan Bogdan. 
The Central Postal Office had four offices: accounting (Neculai Costin), dispatch (Costache Cerchez), day-to-day operations (divided into two sections—Costache Bogdan and Gheorghe Vamis), and a registry containing the archives.
At the central office, there were two dispatch services. The first, led by Cerkez, managed a registry for the sending and receiving of simple and registered letters, as well as newspapers. The second, led by Kostin, handled packages with assigned monetary value, maintaining a separate registry for their dispatch and receipt.
