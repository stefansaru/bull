Russia's vast size and diverse population necessitated a wide range of denominations to accommodate both small and large transactions. Different regions had different economic realities, and having many denominations helped ensure that the currency could be used effectively in various contexts, from small rural markets to large urban centers.
Some of these smaller denominations, like the denga and polushka, were remnants of older currencies from earlier periods of Russian history. The system was a gradual evolution rather than a complete overhaul, which led to the coexistence of old and new coins.
This mix of old coins with newer denominations reflects the gradual modernization of Russian currency.
