### 1858 📅 💰 Lira 🌍 Ottoman Empire

<!-- u:u00494 kind:text -->
Lira 📅 1844  1923

<!-- u:u00497 kind:quad -->
Many Ottoman coins in circulation around 1858 bear the year 1255 in the Islamic (Hijri) calendar because of a specific minting convention used by the Ottoman Empire. In Ottoman coinage, the date inscribed on coins often refers to the accession year of the reigning sultan, rather than the exact year the coin was minted. In this case, 1255 AH (Anno Hegirae) corresponds to 1839 AD, which is the year Sultan Abdülmecid I ascended to the throne. Thus, many coins minted during Sultan Abdülmecid’s reign (which lasted until 1861) carry the accession year 1255 AH, regardless of their actual minting year. To indicate the specific year of minting during the sultan's reign, an additional number known as the regnal year would often be added, typically located below the tughra (the sultan's signature). This regnal year would specify how many years had passed since the sultan’s accession. So, while 1255 AH is frequently seen on coins minted throughout the Sultan’s reign, it does not necessarily mean that the coin was minted in 1839 (1255 AH) but rather that it was issued during his reign, with the exact year of minting indicated by the regnal year. For example, 1255 AH + regnal year 15 would correspond to 1855 AD.
