## 1858 📅 📯 Postal Instructors join D.P.D. 🌍 Iaşi (Jassy)

<!-- u:u00652 kind:text -->
1858-08-31    J📅G    1858-09-12

<!-- u:u00653 kind:quad -->
Postal Instructors join D.P.D.
To implement the reform commission's proposals, the Moldavian government asked Austria to send two officials and two carriage drivers to train the local postal staff and draft rules for running the postal service. Austria accepted, and on August 31, 1858, the Austrian agency in Iași confirmed the delegation's arrival.
Those sent were:
2 Officials: Iochan (Johan) von Manowarda, postal administrator from Bukovina, and Leon Scliba, clerk (oficiant);
2 Carriage Drivers: Ianoș Szergegdi and Frideric Nadler.
Austria stated that all four would remain employees of the Austrian state and be under its protection throughout their one-year mission.
After the year ended, the two drivers and Scliba were withdrawn, but Manowarda stayed on as a "postal instructor," earning 2,600 lei per month. He was later sent on a similar mission to Wallachia.
In October 1858, the "Service Instructions for the Conductors of the Moldavian Post" were drafted and put into effect—a set of 75 articles regulating:
The responsibilities of staff and their behavior toward the administration and the public;
Inspection of mail coaches and the equipment required by drivers;
Handling and transport of letters, money, and parcels;
Managing coaches, stopovers, and delivery of shipments;
Measures in case of service interruptions;
Reporting trips and penalties for negligence or dishonesty.
