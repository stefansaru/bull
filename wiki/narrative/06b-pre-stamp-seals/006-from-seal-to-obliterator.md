## 🖋→⨀ From Seal to Obliterator: the Eve of 1858

The pre-stamp era does not merely precede the *Cap de bour* — it hands directly to
it. In the **spring of 1857**, the diligence entrepreneur **Teodor Ghica** ordered a
set of dies at **Vienna** from the engraver **J. Radnitzky** for the Moldavian
expedition bureaus he managed: the small round **"MOLDOVA" town datestamps**
([[../../entities/handstamps/M1|M1]]) and the straight-line **"RECEPISSE"**
registered-mail mark ([[../../entities/handstamps/C1|C1]]). They were invoiced in the
inventory of **10 November 1857**, drawn up when the state bought back the diligence
privilege — the same buy-back written into the title of the 1858 directorate seal
[[../../entities/handstamps/SM58|SM58]].

These devices were in service **before** any stamp existed — Dragomir shows an
`M1` on a pre-philatelic letter of 1857 — and then, when the *Cap de bour* appeared
on **22 July 1858**, the very same handstamps became its **obliterators**: `M1`
cancelling the stamp, `C1` marking the registered letter, phased in from Iași (22
July 1858) across the Moldavian offices. The large **[[../../entities/handstamps/M2|M2]]**
"MOLDOVA" and the **[[../../entities/handstamps/M3|M3]]** "FRANCO" obliterators
followed in 1859.

So the instruments of the pre-stamp post did not retire when the stamp arrived; they
turned around and cancelled it. The seal-cutter's craft that had authenticated a
menzil order in 1825 was, by 1858, tying a bull's head to a town and a date — the
material of [[../../entities/concepts/bull-head-postmarks-from-documents|the postmark
record]] and the [[../../entities/postmarks/census|postmark census]]. The device
index for the whole pre-stamp world is at
[[../../entities/handstamps/README|entities/handstamps/]].
