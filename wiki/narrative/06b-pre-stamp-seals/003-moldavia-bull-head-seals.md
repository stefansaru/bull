## 🐂 Moldavia's Bull's Head — on the Post's Own Seals

Here the pre-stamp record touches the heart of this book. The **bull's head** the
1858 stamp made world-famous was not lifted from a museum: it was the working
emblem of the **Moldavian post's own seals** for decades before. Dragomir is exact
about the boundary — the bull's head *"appears for the first time **in philately**
on the cap de bour stamps"* — but on the **seals** it was in continuous postal use
long before that ([[../../entities/concepts/moldavia-heraldry|Moldavian heraldry]]).

Three devices tell the arc:

- [[../../entities/handstamps/SM30|**SM30**]] (1830) — the Menzil-House seal already
  carries the **cap de bour with a star between the horns** and flanking branches:
  the arms of the land on the relay-post's own die.
- [[../../entities/handstamps/SM58|**SM58**]] (1858) — struck in the very year of the
  stamp, the seal of the **Directorate of Posts and Diligences** shows the full
  achievement: a shield bearing the bull's head, a closed crown, two flanking
  dolphins, and a postal horn dated 1858, legended *"DIREKȚIA POSTELOR ȘI A
  DELIJANSELOR"*. Its title — *and of the Diligences* — records the moment the state
  absorbed the diligence enterprise on the eve of the stamp.
- **SM59** (1858/59) — after the Union, the Iași bureau seal sets the **Moldavian
  bull's head beside the Wallachian eagle** under *"PRINCIPATELE UNITE"*, prefiguring
  the joint arms of the later *Principatele Unite* stamps.

So when Moldavia reached for a design in 1858, it reproduced the charge its post
handled every day. The stamp is the latest voice of a device running from the
Maramureș seals of the 1380s, through Petru Mușat's groat, to the menzil seal of
1830 ([[../../entities/concepts/aurochs-symbolism|aurochs symbolism]]).
