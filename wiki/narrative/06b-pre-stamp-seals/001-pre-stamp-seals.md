# 🖋 Before the Stamp — Postal Seals of the Two Principalities

> **Draft addendum.** This chapter is newly written from Kiriac Dragomir's
> *Ștampilografie Poștală (România: 1822–1910)* and is **outside the generated
> narrative pipeline** (it has no `source_blocks` into the manuscript `.docx`). It
> cites the concept graph; fold it into the manuscript when ready. See
> [[../../entities/concepts/pre-stamp-postal-markings|the anchor concept]].

The *Cap de bour* of 22 July 1858 was the first stamp a letter-writer in the
Principalities could **buy and stick on**. It was not the first mark the post put on
a letter. For a full generation before it — from **1822** — the mails of
**Moldavia** and **Wallachia (Țara Românească)** were struck by **seals and
handstamps**: the devices of the relay houses, the postal directorates, the
quarantine cordon and the town bureaus. They are the evidential floor beneath the
Bull's Head, and — as we shall see — they already carried the very emblem the stamp
made famous.

The record and its ordering are owed to one book:
[[../../entities/references/dragomir-stampilografie-1990|Kiriac Dragomir's
*Ștampilografie Poștală*]] (1990), the second great stage of Romanian marking-study
after Racoviceanu's 1963 survey. Dragomir reads every impression as **emblem +
legend**, classes the devices by a compact code, and — crucially for a book about
the bull's head — states the heraldic rule plainly: *the principal element of the
seals of Moldavia is the cap de bour, and of Wallachia the eagle*
([[../../entities/concepts/pre-stamp-postal-markings|pre-stamp markings]]).
