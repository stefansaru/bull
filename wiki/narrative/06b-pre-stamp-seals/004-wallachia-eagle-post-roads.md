## 🦅 Wallachia's Eagle and the Seven Post Roads

Across the Milcov the story is the same in shape and different in emblem. Wallachia's
seals carry the **cross-bearing eagle (*acvila cruciată*)**, usually in flight above
a **post-cart drawn by horses** with a whipping postillion — a small moving scene of
the service itself. The central-administration seals
[[../../entities/handstamps/SV27|**SV27**]] (*Casa Menzilurilor*, 1827) and
[[../../entities/handstamps/SV33|**SV33**]] (*Casa Poștelor*, 1833) both show it; the
state-expedition seal [[../../entities/handstamps/SV30|**SV30**]] (*Expediția
Principatului Valahii*) rides on the passport-permits (*podorojne*) that authorised
travel by post-horse.

Behind the seals lies a road map. From Bucharest ran **seven post roads** to the
frontiers — Focșani (branching to Brăila), Călărași (Știrbei from 1853), Giurgiu,
Craiova (with five branches), Câineni, Tîrgoviște–Câmpulung, and Ploiești (pushed to
Predeal in 1854) — their terminal points either Danube ports, which from **1830**
became **quarantines**, or Carpathian customs. Relay stations stood roughly every 16
km under a *căpitan de poștă*; Dragomir illustrates the whole network for the first
five-year lease (1836–1840). The leases themselves stepped up from two-year to
three-year (the *trienale* of 1830 and 1833) to the five-year exploitations of 1836,
1841, 1846, 1851 and 1856 — the last extended to 1 May 1862, when the post finally
passed into direct state management ([[../../entities/handstamps/SV27|SV27]];
[[../../entities/concepts/menzil-relay-system|menzil relay post]]).
