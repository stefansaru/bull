## 🩺 The Quarantine Cordon and the Franco Marks

Two further families of pre-stamp marking sit beside the office seals, and both are
inseparable from how mail actually moved.

The first is the **quarantine post**. From **1 January 1830** a cordon of Danube
**quarantines** (*carantine*) was thrown along the frontier — twelve at the outset —
each principality administering its own, with stations at Calafat, Giurgiu,
Cerneți / Schela-Cladovei, Zimnicea and elsewhere. Letters carried across from
plague- and cholera-country were **disinfected** and struck with the quarantine
office's mark, class **`K`** in Dragomir's key. These are as much sanitary history
as postal history — the point at which epidemic control and the letter post met
([[../../entities/concepts/quarantine-posts|quarantine posts]]).

The second is the **Franco / Porto** family — the **`FM`** (Moldavia) and **`FV`**
(Wallachia) handstamps that recorded whether a letter was **prepaid** (*FRANCO*) or
to be **collected** (*PORTO*) — the accounting logic the adhesive stamp would soon
absorb into a single gummed token
([[../../entities/concepts/franco-porto-handstamps|Franco/Porto handstamps]];
[[../../entities/concepts/franco-porto-apparatus-1858|the 1858 franco/porto
apparatus]]).
