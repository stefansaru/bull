## ⨀ C1a „RECEPISSE” 📅 1859

<!-- u:u00870 kind:text -->
📖 ⨀ Kiriac, p.261

<!-- u:u00871 kind:quad -->
The C1a “RECEPISSE” handstamps are complementary markings used on registered letters, similar in function to the C1 type but noticeably larger. They were ordered at the same time as the “MOLDOVA 2” (M2) handstamps, following Postal Directorate Report no. 2117 of 27 August addressed to the Ministry of Finance. In this report, the Austrian postal advisors—Johann von Manowarda and three colleagues—recommended introducing modern handstamps, in line with the European postal system, for the offices of Reni, Ismail, Agiud, Târgu-Frumos, Cahul, Chilia, Bolgrad, Târgu-Neamț and Târgu-Ocni. In addition, they requested an extra handstamp for Jassy (Iași), needed both for official parcels and private packets, which required separate handling.
The order for the new handstamps was placed with the firm “Witner & Reichel” on 17 September 1858, and in April 1859 they were invoiced at 6.39 lei each. Distribution took place gradually: Iași on 1 April 1859; Adjud, Bolgrad, Cahul, Ismail, Târgu Neamț and Târgu Ocna on 27 April 1859; and Târgu Frumos on 5 May 1859. The offices of Reni and Chilia subsequently relinquished their handstamps, which were reassigned to Iași (type 2) on 29 September 1859 and to Focșani on 6 October 1859.
The C1a handstamps appear exclusively on registered letters, always used together with the M2 handstamps. They were officially withdrawn from service by Order no. 3147 of 1/13 June 1865 and, like the C1 type, were replaced by the C7 “RECOMANDAT” handstamps.
