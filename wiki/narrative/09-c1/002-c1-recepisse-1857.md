## ⨀ C1 „RECEPISSE” 📅 1857

<!-- u:u00863 kind:text -->
📖 Kiriac 1974, p.34

<!-- u:u00864 kind:text -->
📖 ⨀ Kiriac, p.261

<!-- u:u00866 kind:text -->
Figure 265, p.294: 108 Parale △ ⨀ C1

<!-- u:u00867 kind:quad -->
Registered letters are postal items delivered directly to the addressee. This type of service provides extra security for correspondence, as it also includes proof of delivery.
C1 straight-line postmarks are complementary marks used to clearly indicate that a letter was sent as registered mail, meaning that the sender received proof of posting (a receipt of dispatch).
The C1 “RECEPISSE” postmarks were ordered in 1857 in Vienna, from the engraver J. Radnitzky, at the initiative of Teodor Ghica, for the dispatch service of the Moldavian Diligence Company, which he administered. Their first documented mention dates from 10 November 1857, in the inventory prepared when the diligence service was transferred to state control.
The use of the C1 “RECEPISSE” postmarks was mandatory in the 14 dispatch offices, as complementary markings applied to registered and prepaid letters. Their introduction was gradual: in Iași starting on 22 July 1858, in another 12 Moldavian offices (excluding Galați) on 4 August 1858, and in Galați on 1 September 1858.
In all known cases, the C1 postmark was applied together with the M1 datestamp, the latter being the one that normally cancelled the postage stamps. Only in exceptional situations was the C1 postmark used to obliterate the stamps, a notable example being a 108 Parale fragment offered at the Harmers auction, London, 1991.
The C1 postmarks were officially withdrawn from use by Order no. 3147 of 1/13 June 1865 and were replaced by the C7 “RECOMANDAT” (“Registered”) postmarks, introduced beginning on 11 February 1865.
