Chapter I
THE CLASSIC ROMANIAN STAMPS 1858–1872
MOLDAVIA (1858–1862)
UNITED PRINCIPALITIES (1862–1864)

The first Romanian postage stamps, the "BULL'S HEAD" [CAP DE BOUR], were issued on 15 July 1858, as a consequence of the rules laid down by the PROJECT FOR THE REFORM OF THE POSTS, "drawn up by the commission appointed by His Excellency the Prince-Regent [caimacam] and sanctioned by the Administrative Council of Moldavia, to take effect from 1 May 1858" (M.F. no. 1760 of 22 February 1858, A.S.B. fond D.P.D. file 1040/1858 ff. 37–68).

On 1 May 1858 (under no. 975), the Directorate of Posts and Stagecoaches asked the Ministry of Finance to have the stamps manufactured, in conformity with the "INSTRUCTIONS FOR THE CONVEYANCE OF LETTERS", the first three values being mentioned: 27 PARAS, 54 PARAS and 81 PARAS; subsequently decisions were also taken regarding the fourth value, 108 PARAS.

On 26 May 1858 (no. 4258), the Ministry of Finance decided that the stamps should be printed at the STAMP WORKSHOP [ATELIA TIMBRULUI] in Jassy [Iași], approving the ordering of "the dies for those stamps".

On 6 July 1858, the Directorate of Posts and Stagecoaches (under no. 1525) published, "for public knowledge, the rules established for letter postage and the movement of passengers by stagecoach from 1 July 1858", throughout Moldavia (Official Bulletin no. 54).

THE TARIFF OF POSTAL CHARGES FOR LETTERS (Lit. A), adapted according to weight and distance, provides for the charges payable either "by affixing stamps" (franco – on posting) or in cash (postage due – on receipt by the addressee), according to the following scale valid between 1 May – 31 October 1858 (distance in poste, weight in drams, charge in lei and paras). The values as transcribed from the original, in order: 27; 14; 2.5; 2.5; 14; 28; 7.5; 7.5; 28; 16; 15; 15; 20; 15; 29; 30; 18; 20; 40; 10; 16; 32; 30; 12; 40; 60; 80; 30; 13; 20; 60; 80; 17; 14; 34; 120; 120; 160; 31; 200; 22; 160; 18; 18; 250; 200; 30.

and so on, 27 paras for each additional 50 drams (one dram = 3.227 g) and up to 8 poste (one poștă = 7,000 princely fathoms [stânjeni] × 2.23 m, or approximately 15.6 km in theory), the charge being in all cases doubled beyond 8 poste.

Prepaid (franco) letters were handed in directly at the dispatch offices so that the correct application of the tariff could be verified and they could be entered in the accounts (5); for registered letters, with RETURN RECEIPT [RETUR RECIPISSE] (called strahovoi), the prescribed charge was double (6).

Unpaid letters "were dropped into the iron letter boxes with locks" or, in their absence, directly at the dispatch offices (2), post stations (captains) and any other localities (8).

The letter carriers received, in addition, from the addressee, 5 paras for each letter without distinction.

Newspapers were sent wrapped through the parcel post, for which the charge was paid in cash, per Tariff B. (or 10 paras a sheet).

The setting of the postal tariff "in paras" was determined by Turkish influence in the Romanian lands during that period, although other currencies (the French franc, the Austrian florin, etc.) also circulated in the Principalities. The para is a subdivision of the Turkish piastre (40 paras = 1 piastre). One leu corresponded to 40 paras, which meant: 54 PARAS = 1 leu and 14 paras, 81 PARAS = 2 lei and 1 para, and 108 PARAS = 2 lei and 28 paras (or one gold franc).

DELIVERY OF THE CLICHÉS. On 1 July 1858 the Ministry of Finance (under communication no. 5895) informed "His Honour the Secretary of the Stamp Workshop" in Jassy that "four requisite dies had been manufactured", with which 24,000 stamps were to be printed, apportioned "in accordance with the tariffs drawn up", namely: 6,000 pcs. of 27 PARAS; 10,000 pcs. of 54 PARAS; 2,000 pcs. of 81 PARAS and 6,000 pcs. of 108 PARAS.

The clichés (Fig. 37) are round in shape. They are engraved in steel and each has two grooves, by means of which

they were fixed into the head (support) of the printing machine. The manufacture of the clichés cost 389 lei.

Fig. 37

THE PRINTING of the "BULL'S HEAD" stamps was carried out at the STAMP WORKSHOP in Jassy, established in January 1856 by Prince Grigore Ghica, in order to print the so-called "PRINCELY STAMP" [TIMBRU DOMNESC]. In March 1857 a new "press with balance-lever and counter" was bought from Vienna, and to install it "a table with iron screws" was made in the country (A.S.B. fond M.F. file 394/1857).

The sheet contained four rows of eight stamps each, of which, viewed along one of the horizontals, the first two upper rows are normal, the bull's head having its muzzle downward, while the other two rows, in the lower part, have the design reversed, the bull being inverted, with the muzzle upward and the horns downward (Fig. 38). Printed thus, the sheet formed at its centre eight pairs of stamps turned through 180 degrees (horn to horn), known in philately as "tête-bêche".

The parallelism of these stamps could be maintained if the printer was careful. As regards the distance between the stamps, this could be at times greater, at times smaller, sometimes reaching even overlapping. Since visibility was hindered, the position for printing the stamp was determined more by estimation (see also the 2nd issue, p. 35, and the 1862 "United Principalities" issue, p. 39).

For the manner of printing the first "BULL'S HEAD" stamps see p. 35.

THE PAPER USED. For the printing of the first Romanian postage stamps no special paper was ordered. A paper of foreign origin was used, procured through the local shops in Jassy (J.D. MAUGSCH).

In structure, the coloured paper, thin and transparent, is plain [wove] for the 81 PARAS stamp, while for the other values it is crossed by horizontal lines, laid lines [vergeures], which sometimes differ from one sheet to another, uneven in thickness and not always at the same spacing.

The colour of the paper used differs from one value to another, and sometimes there are shade variations even within the same value. The shade differences of the paper found within the same value may also arise from the storage conditions of the stamps or from the influence of various natural factors.

THE GUMMING. As a binder for gumming the sheets, "gummi arabicum" was used. The gum arabic was un-neutralized, insufficiently purified, yellow-brown in colour, applied by hand with a "gum brush",

<!-- pdf page 30 -->

primitively and unevenly. Today, the gum surviving on a very small number of unwashed stamps appears as a hard crust, wholly brittle and cracked into a cobweb pattern, attacking over time the structure of the paper and the colour of the cancellations made with ink of vegetable composition (indigo).

PLACING INTO CIRCULATION. First batch. "So that the operation of stamping private letters (of Moldavia – Author's Note) could begin at least from the 15th of the current month", the Stamp Workshop deposited into the treasury of the Ministry of Finance the first batch of stamps: 187 sheets of 32 stamps, in all 5,984 stamps, of which 992 stamps of 27 PARAS; 992 of 54 PARAS; 480 of 81 PARAS and 3,520 of 108 PARAS (A.T. no. 62 of 11 July 1858). The next day, the stamps were sent to the Directorate of Posts (M.F. no. 6313 of 12 July 1858), for which, on 14 July, the cashier's office of the Directorate of Posts issued a "Certificate of receipt".

The second batch (and the last) took place on 21 July 1858 (A.T. Report 64), when the remainder of 18,080 stamps was deposited at the Ministry of Finance (5,024/27 PARAS; 9,024/54 PARAS; 1,536/81 PARAS and 2,496/108 PARAS), and the latter, on 24 July, sent them to the Directorate of Posts.

Although 15 July 1858 is considered the date of their issue, the "BULL'S HEAD" stamps were first placed into circulation at the central dispatch office of JASSY a week later. From the recently discovered register, entitled "Protocol of registered and simple letters and of parcels, as well as newspapers received and dispatched in the course of the year 1858" (A.S.B. file 6997/1858), it appears that on Tuesday 22 July 1858 the first 16 simple letters and 7 registered ones were prepaid, bearing nos. 992–998 (A.17); then follows 4 August at the other offices, with the exception (1 September) of GALATZ [Galați] and TG. NEAMȚ (without a handstamp).

WITHDRAWAL FROM CIRCULATION. In the course of September 1858 it was decided to change the postal tariff to a zonal one and to return to the old single postal tariff, effective from 1 November of the same year.

In practice the "bull's head" stamps of the first issue were able to circulate for 102 days in the capital of Moldavia (Jassy) and 89 days in the district seats (with the exception of GALATZ, 61 days).

The quantities of unsold stamps, as well as those withdrawn from circulation, were taken over by the central cashier of the Jassy Directorate of Posts, the steward NICOLAE COSTIN. On his death, 12,318 stamps were found (D.P./584 of 26 February 1859), which were deposited for safekeeping in the treasury of the Finance Department of Moldavia (M.F./2878 of 10 March 1859). From 10 March 1859 nothing concrete is any longer known about the fate of these stamps: it is thought that, at a liquidation of accounts, they were destroyed between 1859 and 1862.

"THE MOROIU FORGERIES". In 1888, C.M. Moroiu produced, in his clandestine workshop in Bucharest, a number of forgeries of all the BULL'S HEAD values, of which the 81 PARAS on white laid paper and the other three values on white, plain [wove] paper.

"THE GORJAN REPRINT". In 1891 there were found in the storehouse of the Bucharest post office the four original clichés with which the first Romanian BULL'S HEAD/1858 stamps, first issue, had been printed. The Director of Posts and Telegraphs, Colonel August Gorjan (in office 26 Feb. 1891 – 22 Feb. 1892), ordered the hand-printing, with these now-worn dies applied directly onto white, lightly laid paper, in groups of three pieces of each value, of a limited number of facsimiles, in black ink.

"NEWSPAPER POSTAGE" [PORTO GAZETEI], an unused cliché (fig. 39). At the Stamp Factory in Bucharest there was discovered in 1944 a cliché similar to those of the first "BULL'S HEAD" stamps. The cliché is round; it bears the legend PORTO GAZETEI

in Latin letters. The bull with the five-pointed star. In the loop of the horn is inscribed the value, the figure 10 and the letter P: the abbreviation of the word paras. The cliché was proposed to the Ministry of Finance of Moldavia through the communication of the Jassy Directorate of Posts no. 2227 of 12 September 1858, for the printing of stamps needed for "the franking of newspapers at the value of 10 paras each". No such stamps were ever printed, since a reduction of the charges for sending newspapers and publications by post to 5 paras had been foreseen.

REPRINTS. The theme with the arms of Moldavia was taken up again: in 1932, through the issue "JUBILEE OF 75 YEARS SINCE THE APPEARANCE OF THE BULL'S HEAD STAMPS"; in 1950, when the souvenir sheet "PHILATELIC EXHIBITION" was issued, bearing printed in facsimile, at the left, the 81 PARAS stamp and next to it a 10 LEI franking stamp with the arms of the R.P.R. [People's Republic of Romania]; in 1958, on the occasion of the "CENTENARY OF ROMANIAN STAMPS", when a souvenir sheet was issued bearing in facsimile a 27 PARAS "tête-bêche"; and in 1963, on the occasion of "STAMP DAY 1963", the low value bearing in facsimile the 27 PARAS stamp.

CIRCULATION, CANCELLATIONS. It is considered that, of the philatelic surviving stock of postage stamps of the first "BULL'S HEAD" issue, over 85% are in cancelled condition. Particular attention is therefore required also in the identification of the handstamps applied and the inks used for cancellation, during the four months of circulation of these stamps: July, August, September and October.

The stamps had franking validity only in Moldavia and may bear cancellations from 14 localities: 15 July – 31 October (JASSY); 4 August – 31 October (BACĂU, BÎRLAD, BOTOȘANI, DOROHOI, FĂLTICENI, FOCȘANI, HUȘI, MIHĂILENI, PIATRA, ROMAN, TECUCI and VASLUI); and 1 September – 31 October (GALATZ).

The "BULL'S HEAD" stamps of the first issue circulated until 31 October 1858. Where they were lacking in stock before that date, the charge on the mailings was collected in cash (postage due).

HANDSTAMPS USED: a) B.1/MOLDOVA, "double circle" (fig. 40–54). Some of these handstamps have been found on archival documents and unpaid covers dated well before 15 July 1858 (the issue of the stamps), for which reason they may also be regarded as pre-philatelic handstamps (A.17). Ex. "early date" of 1857: JASSY [Iași] 24/6 (COL. 20) and WASLUI [Vaslui] 4/11 (COL. 48).

The appointment of dispatchers is attested also in other localities which, although they handled stamps, did not have handstamps in 1858 (e.g. ADJUD, TG. NEAMȚ, TG. OCNA, TG. FRUMOS, etc.), the cancellation being able to be done at the district (A.17).

The handstamps are round, double circle (21 mm and 13 mm); above, the name of the post office and below, MOLDOVA, in Egyptian capital letters; in the middle, the date, in Arabic numerals, in the form of a fraction, the day in the numerator, the month in the denominator. The 15 Moldavian handstamps were manufactured in Vienna, bearing the name of the office in German spelling: BAKEU (Bacău), BERLAD (Bîrlad), BOTUSCHANI (Botoșani), DOROHOI (Dorohoi), FOKSCHANI (Focșani), FOLTITSCHENI (Fălticeni), GALATZ (Galați), HUSCH (Huși), JASSY (Iași, with 2 handstamps), MICHALENI (Mihăileni), PIATRA (Piatra Neamț), ROMAN (Roman), TEKUTSCH (Tecuci) and WASLUI (Vaslui). Of the JASSY handstamp two types are known, the second having the word MOLDOVA in antiqua letters (without serifs), not found on stamps in 1858.

b) C.3/RECEPISSE. For registered letters, straight-line handstamps without a frame were used, with the inscription "RECEPISSE", in oblique Latin capital letters (fig. 55). They were used for stamping the covers, together with the round MOLDOVA handstamp on the stamps, to specify the type of mailing. According to the height of the letters, these handstamps can be grouped into three categories:
– with a height of about 5 mm: BACĂU (5/27), BOTOȘANI (5/35), DOROHOI and FĂLTICENI (5/28), HUȘI and IAȘI (5/35), MIHĂILENI, PIATRA, ROMAN, VASLUI.
– with a height of about 4 mm: strongly sloping, BÎRLAD (4/27), IAȘI, TG. NEAMȚ, TG. OCNA.
– with a height of about 3 mm: GALATZ.

THE COLOURS OF THE INKS. In the period 15 July – 1 November 1858, the post offices used for cancellations the following kinds of ink colours: blue, green and carmine with their shades. The green-blue or veronese ink colour predominates (commonly called blue); the red-carmine colour (red in the catalogues) is found only at the JASSY office, between 28 August – 18 October. There are no handstamps in black ink.

On examining the archival documents (A.S.B.) of the period 15 July – 31 October 1858, the following ink colours were found: blue (BACĂU, FOCȘANI, GALATZ, IAȘI and ROMAN); light blue (GALATZ); blue-greenish (FOCȘANI); green (FOCȘANI); greenish (DOROHOI, GALATZ); green-blue or veronese (BACĂU, BÎRLAD, BOTOȘANI, DOROHOI, FĂLTICENI, HUȘI, IAȘI, MIHĂILENI, ROMAN, TECUCI, VASLUI); yellowish-green (PIATRA); red-carmine (IAȘI).

INVENTORYING, CATALOGUING. Of the 11,746 "BULL'S HEAD" first-issue specimens sold between 15 July – 31 October 1858, the existence of only a very small number is known today. The first attempt at an inventory was made in 1933 by RENÉ PONCELET. At the 1958 inventory, Dr. L.N. CRISTEA identified 397 specimens, namely: 27 PARAS (10 unused and 89 used), 54 PARAS (12 unused and 122 used), 81 PARAS (26 unused and 18 used) and 108 PARAS (11 unused and 109 used). (The Stamp Lover No. 11/1858)

"BULL'S HEAD" SECOND ISSUE
(1 November 1858 – 5 May 1862)

The application of the postal tariff of 14 February 1857, based on distance and weight, proved over time the difficult manner of calculating the postal charges and the dissatisfaction of the public, who in general did not take distance into account. On the report of the Directorate of Posts, the Ministry of Finance

<!-- pdf page 33 -->

(under communication no. 8,823 of 17 September 1858) addressed itself to the Secretariat of State in order to obtain approval for the simplification of the postal charges. A few days later (under no. 4284 of 23 September 1858), the Secretariat of State of Moldavia replied to the Ministry of Finance that the introduction of the single tariff was approved, to take effect from 1 November 1858 (see the single tariff in the Lexicon).

Under no. 9,740 of 13 October the Ministry of Finance placed the first order with the Stamp Workshop in Jassy for the urgent printing of a batch of 20,000 stamps (5,000 pcs. of 80 PARAS for registered letters; 12,000 pcs. of 40 PARAS for simple letters and 3,000 pcs. of 5 PARAS for the franking of newspapers).

The Stamp Workshop, on 18 October, requested "the dies" from the Ministry of Finance and on 27 October delivered the first batch of 10,816 stamps (960/5 PARAS; 7,040/40 PARAS and 2,816/80 PARAS). Under no. 10,283 of 27 October 1858 the Ministry of Finance forwarded them to the Directorate of Posts for distribution and use.

The new postal charges and the corresponding stamps were made known on 30 October 1858 through the government official gazette GAZETA DE MOLDAVIA, of Jassy: "It is publicly made known that the payment for letters, according to the high decree of the 1st of the month of November, has been established at an equality for any distance here in the Principality, at one leu, at the Treasury's rate, per weight of 2 1/2 drams; while registered letters are subject to double payment. In accordance with such provision, there having been printed, namely, stamps of one leu and two lei, these will be found for sale and in stock at the same places from the said day." The Treasury rate of 1 leu was equivalent to 40 paras; however, a coin of 1 leu did not exist in 1858; while 2.5 drams is about 8 grams.

THE DESIGN. For the clichés of the "BULL'S HEAD" second-issue stamps, a new design was chosen: the round frame is replaced by a rectangular frame, with the base somewhat smaller than the height. The bull's head and the post horn have a different form; the star between the horns has six points instead of five; the legend is written in Latin letters, the Cyrillic ones being retained only for the word paras, abbreviated PAR, which, moreover, appears for the first time on stamps.

THE PRINTING OF THE "BULL'S HEAD" second-issue stamps was likewise carried out at the STAMP WORKSHOP in Jassy (with the exception of the 5 PAR. NEWSPAPER POSTAGE value, broken frame).

By studying whole sheets of 5 PARAS and 40 PARAS (Fig. 56), the hypothesis of the printing process was confirmed, similar to the stamps of the first "BULL'S HEAD" issue, each stamp being printed separately, the sheet being made up of the respective 32 stamps

<!-- pdf page 34 -->

in four rows of eight pieces, of which the marginal rows separately and the central rows separately. The 16 stamps in the central rows are arranged in eight "tête-bêche" pairs, sheets being found with these rows printed lighter in colour than the two marginal rows (COL. 38, lot 1332), whence the well-known vertical pairs in which one stamp is darker and the second lighter in colour.

THE PAPER USED is of superior quality (pelure, of English origin – the firm BATH-SOMERSET). The paper is plain [wove], white or faintly azured to bluish; the white paper is also found in a yellowish shade.

From the data extracted from the existing documents, repeated print runs emerge (about 14, the first of 27.10.1858 and the last of 26.11.1861), totalling 23,040 pcs. of 5 PARAS; 106,032 pcs. of 40 PARAS and 60,072 pcs. of 80 PARAS.

THE PLACING INTO CIRCULATION OF THE "BULL'S HEAD" STAMPS, second issue, coincides with the day the new postal tariff came into force (1 November 1858) and only through 16 Moldavian localities. The offices of FOCȘANI, GALATZ, IAȘI and VASLUI received and used stamps of all three values (5, 40 and 80 PARAS); the other offices: BACĂU, BÎRLAD, BOTOȘANI, DOROHOI, FĂLTICENI, HUȘI, MIHĂILENI, PIATRA, ROMAN, TECUCI, etc. received stamps only of the values 40 and 80 PARAS.

THE WITHDRAWAL FROM CIRCULATION was officially announced for 1 May 1862. Some post offices (IAȘI, BOTOȘANI, FĂLTICENI, MIHĂILENI), however, used these stamps until 5 May 1862, five days later than the publication of the new postal charges and of the withdrawal order (No. 1662 of 30 April 1862). Between 1 May – 25 June 1862, it was decided that the handling of letters be done without stamps; the postal charges were collected in cash by the dispatchers, the amount collected being written on the cover (order of the D.P. Jassy, no. 21 of 2 May 1862).

HANDSTAMPS USED. For stamping the correspondence of November 1858 – May 1862, three categories of Moldavian handstamps of new manufacture were used.

B.3/FRANCO (fig. 57–74). On the BULL'S HEAD stamps the word "PORTO" was wrongly inscribed, which in commercial terms means payment of the cost of carriage by the addressee, whereas, since that charge is paid by the sender, together with the affixing of postage stamps the word "FRANCO" ought to have been indicated. This was achieved by applying a special handstamp to the stamps.

<!-- pdf page 35 -->

The FRANCO handstamp appears in 1859; it has a frame whose shape differs for each post office, "in order to stamp out abuses through the reuse of stamps" (M.F. no. 361 of 30 Nov. 1859). The spelling of the locality differs in most cases from that of the round MOLDOVA handstamps.

The post offices of IAȘI and GALATZ used two types of handstamp each. JASSY (type 2) and MIHĂILENI are considered rare.

B.2/MOLDOVA-large (fig. 75–80). Following the report of the D.P. Jassy (2,117 of 28 August 1858), on 27 April 1859 the Post of Moldavia was equipped with a further ten round handstamps, with a double circle, of the type of the first round MOLDOVA handstamps but larger, with the outer circle 25 mm in diameter and the inner one 13 mm. The locality above, two demarcation flowers and MOLDOVA below, with taller (4 mm) and bolder characters; the date in the middle in the form of a common fraction, in Arabic numerals. Examples: ADJUD, FOKSCHANI (1861), JASSY type 2, with an acanthus leaf (for simple and registered letters, on arrival), JASSY type 1, with a narrow flower (for insured-registered letters), T. FRUMOS, T. OKNEI, T. NEAMZU, etc.

B.4 and B.5/FOKSCHANI without "MOLDOVA". Beginning on 3 January 1862 the small handstamp B.1/FOKSCHANI × MOLDOVA, and from 30 December 1861 the large handstamp B.2/FOKSCHANI × MOLDOVA, appeared without the word "MOLDOVA", becoming, respectively, B.4 (Fig. 81) and B.5 (Fig. 82) and used as such until June 1865 (A.17). Ex. "Late date": B.5 on the 20 PARAS "Cuza" of 23/2 (COL. 20). See also p. 72, Fig. 136.

B.1 + B.2/MOLDOVA. The FRANCO handstamp, which was applied to the stamps, was mute; it was ordered that, in addition to this handstamp, the letter should also bear the round handstamp B.1/MOLDOVA (small) or B.2/MOLDOVA (large), with the locality and the date. The rule was more or less observed, yet the second-issue BULL'S HEAD stamps were also sometimes cancelled with round handstamps alone.

C.3/RECEPISSE, known since 1858, continued to be used for cancelling registered letters, together with the two handstamps FRANCO (on the stamps) and MOLDOVA (on the cover).

"THE GORJAN REPRINT". In 1891 the Director of Posts, General GORJAN, ordered the printing of a limited number of facsimiles, applying the original dies of the "BULL'S HEAD" second issue, found in the archive, directly onto white paper or laid paper, in black ink. Known are the 5 PARAS on laid paper, the 40 and 80 PARAS on plain [wove] paper (COL. 69/album 34).

"UNITED PRINCIPALITIES"
(26 June 1862 – 31 December 1864)

On 1 May 1862 the new postal tariff officially came into force, on the basis of which the third issue of classic Romanian stamps was prepared. They are known in philately under the name of "the United Principalities stamps", the first to have franking and circulation validity throughout both united Romanian Principalities.

This tariff is characterized by a considerable reduction of all charges, both on letters

<!-- pdf page 36 -->

and on newspapers and printed matter; likewise, also by the introduction for the first time of a special charge for the service within the same locality (loco-transport), while at the same time the per-kilometre zones were abolished (whence the name "single tariff").

The introduction of the new postal tariff and the transfer to the state of the administration of the posts under direct management [regie] was effected by decision no. 7,390 of 27 April 1862 of the Ministry of Finance (Monitor no. 93/1862), while the rules concerning franking and the handing in of correspondence were given through circular no. 14,875 of 28 July of the D.G.P. [General Directorate of Posts] (Monitor no. 168/1862).

"For greater facilitation of postal correspondence, that is, so that each person may frank his letter without needing to present himself at the dispatch office, the government has deemed it necessary to adopt the system of stamps as well as letter boxes. The stamps that have been adopted are of three values:
a) stamps to the value of three paras each for printed sheets;
b) stamps to the value of six paras each for local letter correspondence, and
c) stamps to the value of thirty paras each for out-of-town letter correspondence.
The sale of the stamps is made at the post office, where anyone can procure them, as well as at other private places that will soon be indicated by a separate notice.
The post office carriers are likewise obliged to procure stamps for any person who requests them, the carrier being paid by the buyer only the proper value of the stamps."

The charge on letters for their circulation within the interior of the country, whatever the distance, was fixed by TARIFF no. 1, as follows:

| Weight | Prepaid letters | Unpaid letters |
|---|---|---|
| from 1 to 5 drams | 15 paras | 30 paras |
| from 5 to 10 drams | 30 paras | — |
| from 10 to 50 drams | 10 | — |
| from 50 to 100 drams | — | — |
| from 100 to 150 drams | 12 | 14 |
| from 150 to 200 drams | 18 | 20 |

and so on.

The post also accepts registered letters. In such a case they must necessarily be prepaid on posting, and for registration the charge of a simple letter of 5 drams (30 paras) will be paid in addition, whatever the weight of the registered letter (fixed charge).

If the sender requests that the receipt for the delivery of a registered letter be brought to him, he will pay for this too, separately, again the charge of a simple letter (that is, 30 paras).

For simple loco-transport letters, for the capital (town post) and which must always be prepaid, one stamp of the 6-para value will be applied for every ten drams.

The charge on printed sheets, under wrapper, sent by private persons, is three paras per sheet, of the size of a leaf, but these may not carry letters with them, only the address, the date and the signature of the sender. The charge on these sheets is paid on posting them, by affixing stamps to the wrapper, one stamp for each sheet.

Although the new postal tariff, now drawn up in Bucharest, came into force on 1 May 1862, the "United Principalities postage stamps" could not be placed into circulation at that time, but with a delay of eight weeks, that is, on 26 June 1862 in Wallachia (e.g. the Bucharest office) and much later in Moldavia (15 July 1862). During this interval, the franking charges were paid in cash, per Order 7,388 of 27 April 1862 of the D.P., followed also by telegram no. 1,662 of 30 April 1862: "From 1 May, the handling of prepaid letters shall be done without stamps, until the sending of new stamps…". Thus, in the period from 1 May until the arrival of the new postage stamps, the charges were paid in cash, the correspondence circulating without stamps.

THE DESIGN OF THE "UNITED PRINCIPALITIES" STAMPS presents the following features: the frame border is apparently square, formed of two parallel lines (the inner one very fine), which sometimes merge over shorter or longer stretches through blots of colour. The corners of the frame are cut obliquely.

The central design symbolizes, through the heraldic attributes, the arms of the two principalities: THE BULL'S HEAD (Moldavia) and THE EAGLE (Wallachia). The text FRANCO SCRISORI (on the lateral parts) and PAR (below the horn and above the coats of arms) is written entirely in Latin characters; the star between the bull's horns has five points, like the BULL'S HEAD stamps of the first issue. The eagle has a crown on its head and a cross in its beak.

THE PRINTING. In December 1861/January 1862 the "Stamp Workshop" of Jassy began moving to Bucharest the equipment, inventory and specialist personnel. After assembly, it resumed activity under the name "Autograph Press" [Presa Autografă], subordinate to the Ministry of Finance, the issuer of fiscal instruments.

The printing of the "UNITED PRINCIPALITIES" stamps was carried out by two different processes, thus creating two categories of stamps.

"HAND-PRESS PRINTING" [TIPAR DE MÂNĂ] (Fig. 83). In the period April 1862 – September 1864 the stamps were produced with the old "manual press" in sheets of 32 pcs., using as a matrix a "die" [stemplu] for each stamp and value individually; they are catalogued "UNITED PRINCIPALITIES" ISSUE I/1862 (known in philately also under the improper name of "hand-press" stamps). Thus in this issue too, eight "tête-bêche" pairs resulted and, likewise, vertical pairs can be found in which one stamp has a different shade of colour from the other (COL. 10).

"MACHINE PRINTING" [TIPAR DE MAȘINĂ] (Fig. 84). In the period September – December 1864, the stamps were printed with new machines, using as a matrix a plate of 40 electrotype clichés for each sheet and value; they are catalogued "UNITED PRINCIPALITIES" ISSUE II/1864 (known in philately also under the name of "machine print" or "typographed stamps").

THE PAPER USED was of several kinds and qualities, of different structure, plain [wove] or horizontally laid (the first print runs of the 1862 issue). The sheet size is about 204 × 130 mm; in thickness, from very thin to thin paper of various thicknesses. According to the shade of colour of the various paper qualities, the following are distinguished: white, yellowish, grey and bluish (only for the 30 PARAS). The paper was of English origin (e.g. BATH). Stamps are known of the 3 PARAS/1862 and 30 PARAS/1862 values, with a manufacturer's watermark in capital letters: C of dimensions 11.5/5.5 mm, or respectively L (followed by a hyphen).

THE COMPOSITION OF THE GUM. Gum arabic was used, un-neutralized, insufficiently purified, applied with a brush in a primitive and uneven manner, yellow-brown in colour. Over the years the gum attacks the paper structure of the stamp and the colour of the cancellations made with ink of vegetable composition.

THE ORGANIZATION OF THE POSTS AND THE CIRCULATION OF THE STAMPS

With the appearance of the "UNITED PRINCIPALITIES" stamps there took place a sustained campaign of reorganization of the postal service. The most notable achievement was the unification of the posts of Moldavia and Wallachia, effected by Decree 527 of 23 July 1862, within the framework of the measures taken for the unification of all the institutions and services of Bucharest and Jassy.

The new organization of the unified postal service was regulated by Decree 615 of 7 August 1862, which establishes, among other things: the letter post is separated from the stagecoach service; the postal service, a state monopoly, is exercised directly through state organs; the enlargement of the network of the postal service throughout the country, by the establishment of post offices attached to all the sub-prefectures; private correspondence dispatched from villages, like official correspondence, is to be conveyed from the seat of the prefectures to the central post offices by mounted couriers [dorobanți], the rural post being entrusted to the local state organs.

A measure of particular importance is also the unification of the posts with the telegraph under a single GENERAL DIRECTORATE OF THE TELEGRAPH AND POSTS, decided by the J.C.M. [Council of Ministers Resolution] of 29 August 1864.

Circulation. The "UNITED PRINCIPALITIES" postage stamps were placed on sale on 26 June 1862 in Wallachia and about a month later (after 15 July 1862) in Moldavia. They had franking validity until 31 December 1864, when they were withdrawn from circulation (per the Order of the D.P.T. of 28 December 1864, officially from 1 January 1865, Old Style), a new reduced postal tariff coming into force, for which the "CUZA STAMPS" were issued and placed into circulation. The "UNITED PRINCIPALITIES" stamps remaining in stock (unsold) were liquidated at an auction in 1885.

Design characteristics of the 30 PARALE/1862

The die of the 30 PARALE/1862 value, hand printing, shows fine, complete lines in the text and figures.

The frame is formed of a thin line on the inside and a thick line on the outside. The dimensions of the frame vary with the state of the stamp (gummed or ungummed), the width being 18–18.5 mm and the height 19.5–20 mm.

FRANCO has the letter R with the loop broken from the vertical leg. The letter A with the left leg very fine, wavy and ending vertically on the foot. C has a thick loop and a bevelled lower curve. The letters have dissimilarities in size (e.g. the letter A shorter than the letter N).

SCRISOREI has the first S with the lower tail much extended in the form of a tail and with a concave termination. The first R is inclined to the left (Fig. 118) and joins its small leg to the little foot of the letter I. The second R is likewise fused by its little feet to the following letter E. The foot and bracket of the last I long and concavely wavy; the foot much brought forward.

30 PAR (top) has the figure 3 with the lower curve bevelled, while the figure 0 (zero) much open. The letter P with a long, faintly wavy foot. The first leg of the letter A has a break below the horizontal stroke. The letter R, with wavy outlines, seems inclined toward A.

30 PAR (bottom) has the figure 3 with the lower loop much larger and projecting outward, while the figure 0 (zero) much narrower and with a chip (bevel) in the upper right part. The letter P with the bracket brought round in a wavy manner and the leg very thick. The letter A has the stroke slightly inclined to the left. The long little feet of A and R appear equal.

The eagle with a deformation of the cross of the crown, so that the right arm appears broken, often resembling a blot.

The aurochs has the ears outlined with thick hair, comb-like; the left one is finely attached to the eagle's wing. The eyes with round pupils, like two little circles, and the iris white. The nose outlined by three thick, unequal lines, of which the median one long as far as the nostrils. The star with five sharp points has thicker lines than in the other values.

The mouthpiece in an empty circle, with a white space left by the stop-bar of the two inner lines.

30 PARALE/1864 – TYPOGRAPHED

The 40 dies obtained by electrotyping are not correctly executed. The dimensions of the frame have in general an excess of 0.25 mm and vary with the state of the stamp (gummed or ungummed). The design lines are usually clogged and with breaks (chips). The marks of the nails (with which the dies were fixed to the wooden support) are clearly visible on a large part of the stamps, sometimes in the form of dots, small circles or parts of them. Each stamp in the sheet, moreover, presents its own characteristics (in the design, dotted arrows noted with letters), by means of which we can determine the position of the respective stamp in the sheet (its ordinal number).
